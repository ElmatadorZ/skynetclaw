The prior theory in this lineage (Warrant) asked *when a claim deserves acceptance*.
It presupposed the claim already existed. This theory recovers what comes **before
the claim**: the question. Erotetic logic (Hamblin; Belnap & Steel) is the field.

## Q1 / D1 · What is a question (minimal ontology)

**I1 — A question is not an assertion; it is a *partition of possibility*.** The
recovered core (Hamblin's postulate; Groenendijk–Stokhof): the semantic content of a
question *is the set of its admissible direct answers* — equivalently, a partition of
logical space into the cells the answers pick out. A claim *closes* the space (picks
one cell); a question *opens* it (names the cells worth distinguishing). SUPPORTED.

**I2 — A question carries a presupposition.** Every question presupposes that at
least one of its direct answers is true (Belnap). "Which door hides the prize?"
presupposes a prize behind a door. A question whose presupposition is false is
*defective* — it has no true direct answer ("Have you stopped cheating?" to one who
never cheated). SUPPORTED — presupposition is constitutive, not decorative.

**I3 — Minimal ontology (three parts):**
```
Question Q = ⟨ A , π , ρ ⟩
   A  = the answer-partition (≥2 distinguishable admissible answers)
   π  = the presupposition (that some answer in A holds)
   ρ  = the relevance/aim (why this partition matters — the contrast the asker cares about)
```
Drop A → not a question (nothing is being distinguished). Drop π → nonsensical.
Drop ρ → an idle question (well-formed but pointless). SUPPORTED for A, π; ρ is
LIKELY (van Fraassen's pragmatic theory makes relevance constitutive of *why*-
questions specifically).

**I4 — A question is the recognition-structure for an unknown.** This is the deep
one and the answer to Meno (below): a question specifies *enough about the answer's
shape to recognize it when found*, without containing the answer. It is the mold, not
the casting. SUPPORTED (Bromberger — you can be in the predicament of knowing a
question has an answer, and recognizing the answer on sight, without being able to
produce it).

### Meno's paradox (the paradox of inquiry — the founding problem)
*If you know the answer, inquiry is unnecessary; if you don't, you won't recognize it
— so inquiry is impossible.* The recovered resolution (post-Plato): inquiry runs on
**partial knowledge**. The question (I4) supplies the *criterion of recognition* —
you know the answer's category, form, and success-condition without its value. Meno's
dichotomy (know-all / know-nothing) is false: you know the *question's shape*, which
is exactly the middle state that makes inquiry both possible and necessary. SUPPORTED
(this is why a well-formed question is itself a form of knowledge — knowledge of the
unknown's shape).

## Q2 · Necessary conditions — what makes a "question" a real question

Tested against the defective cases (loaded questions, rhetorical questions, ill-posed
questions, pseudo-questions):

| Candidate condition | Necessary? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Nontrivial partition** (≥2 admissible answers that differ) | **necessary** | a "question" all of whose answers are equivalent partitions nothing — it is rhetorical, not erotetic |
| **Satisfiable presupposition** | **necessary** | a false-presupposition question has no true direct answer (defective) — I2 |
| **Answer-recognizability** (a criterion to know the answer when found) | **necessary** | without it, no possible response counts as *the* answer (Meno; Bromberger) — I4 |
| **In-principle answerability** | necessary for a *live* question; its failure defines a *dead* question (file 04), not a non-question | scope-setting |
| **Relevance to an aim** | necessary for a *good* question, not for a *well-formed* one | quality (file 01), not existence |

**Recovered necessary core:** *nontrivial partition + satisfiable presupposition +
answer-recognizability.* A string with a "?" lacking any of these is a pseudo-
question — grammatically interrogative, epistemically empty. SUPPORTED.

## Q3 / D2 · Taxonomy of questions

The primary cut is by **the shape of the admissible answer** (the erotetic form),
which the given list refines by *aim*:

| Kind (aim) | Erotetic form | Answer shape | Info structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Descriptive** ("what/which/whether") | which-/whether- | a value / a cell | reduces state-uncertainty |
| **Explanatory** ("why") | why-, with a *contrast class* (van Fraassen: "why P rather than Q") | a cause/reason relative to the contrast | reduces model-uncertainty |
| **Predictive** ("what will") | which-, future-indexed | a forecast + its condition | reduces forecast-uncertainty |
| **Counterfactual** ("what if") | which-, under an intervention | an outcome under do(·) | reduces causal-structure uncertainty |
| **Normative** ("what ought") | which-, over an evaluative order | a ranked action/permission | reduces value-uncertainty |
| **Diagnostic** ("what's wrong / which cause") | which-, over a fault space | the actual fault | reduces attribution-uncertainty |
| **Design** ("how to achieve X") | how-, over a means space | a construction/plan | reduces means-uncertainty |
| **Discovery** ("what unknown exists here") | open / generative | a *new* partition itself | reduces *question*-uncertainty (meta) |

Two structural facts about the taxonomy (recovered):
- **Explanatory why-questions are partition-relative (van Fraassen).** "Why P?" is
  ill-formed until the *contrast class* is fixed ("why P rather than Q vs rather than
  R" are different questions). The contrast is part of the question, not the answer.
  SUPPORTED — the most-overlooked structure in ordinary questioning.
- **Discovery questions are second-order.** They ask not for an answer within a
  partition but for a *new partition worth having* — a question about which questions
  to ask. They are where genuinely new knowledge enters (Popper: science starts from
  problems, and the deepest advances *reformulate the problem*). This is the class a
  mere answering-system cannot occupy. LIKELY — and the pivot of the whole mission.

## Falsifiers
I1 refuted if a coherent question is shown to assert (close the space) rather than
partition it. The necessary core is refuted by a genuine, useful question lacking a
nontrivial partition or a satisfiable presupposition. The taxonomy is refuted by a
real question that fits no aim-category and no erotetic form — none is known, but the
list is offered as recovered-and-open, not closed.
