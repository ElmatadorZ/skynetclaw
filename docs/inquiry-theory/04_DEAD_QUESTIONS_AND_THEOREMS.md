## Q7 / D8 · Dead questions — the unanswerable, and how to tell

A **dead question** is one with no obtainable true direct answer. Recovered kinds,
ordered by how *robustly* dead they are:

| Kind | Why dead | Robustly dead? |
|---|---|---|
| **False-presupposition** | no true direct answer *exists* (I2) — "which prime is between 7 and 11 that is even?" | **Yes** — dead in every method-world |
| **Formally undecidable** | provably no algorithm/proof settles it (Gödel; halting) | **Yes** — dead by proof |
| **Ill-posed** | no genuine partition / no exhaustive-exclusive answer set | **Yes** (until reposed) |
| **Empirically inaccessible** | no *possible* observation discriminates the answers (permanent underdetermination) | **Method-relative** |
| **Pseudo-question** | no verification/recognition conditions (Carnap's metaphysical "pseudo-problems") | **CONTESTED** (positivist verdict; many "meaningless" questions later became live) |
| **p-predicament (Bromberger)** | answerable *in principle*, just not by us *now* | **NOT dead — DORMANT** |

**The critical distinction — dead vs dormant.** Only *false-presupposition* and
*formal-undecidability* are robustly dead (dead in every method-world). The rest are
**method-relative**: "empirically inaccessible" means *inaccessible by current
methods*; new instruments revive it (questions about atoms, continents, genes were
all once "unanswerable"). Therefore:

> **Dead-question caveat (SUPPORTED).** Most "this can never be answered" verdicts are
> themselves defeasible claims relative to the current method-space — and are subject
> to Warrant's own decay. Declaring a question *permanently* dead is a strong claim
> requiring its own warrant; the safe default for the method-relative kinds is
> **DORMANT** (parked, revisit on method change), not DEAD. The pseudo-question verdict
> is the least safe of all (positivism's history of premature death-certificates).

**How to tell (the recovered diagnostic):**
1. Presupposition satisfiable? No → *dead* (false-presupposition).
2. Formally decidable? Provably not → *dead*.
3. Any *possible* observation/experiment discriminates the answers? No, ever → *dead
   (permanent underdetermination)*; No, *now* → **dormant**.
4. Recognition criterion exists (I4)? No → *ill-posed* — repose it before pronouncing.

Distinguishing dead from dormant is itself a high-value question (file 01): mislabeling
a dormant question dead forecloses inquiry (a false stop); mislabeling a dead question
live wastes cost forever (frontier-thrash). LIKELY.

## Q10 / D9 · Theorems

Consolidated recovered results of the theory of inquiry:

| # | Theorem | Status | Falsifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Q-part** | A question is a partition of possibility + a presupposition + an aim | SUPPORTED | a question that asserts rather than partitions |
| **Meno-res** | Inquiry is possible via partial knowledge — the question is the recognition-shape of an unknown answer | SUPPORTED | a genuine inquiry with zero prior grasp of the answer's shape |
| **EIG** | A question's epistemic value = mutual information of its answer with the target (per cost, per aim) | SUPPORTED | a valuable question whose answer is independent of the target |
| **E (frontier)** | Answers evoke questions; the question-graph expands as knowledge grows | LIKELY | an empirical domain where answering evokes none |
| **Stop** | Rational stop = earliest of {sufficiency, VoI<cost, exhaustion, sequential-boundary} | SUPPORTED | a case where continuing past all four is rational |
| **Dead** | Only false-presupposition & undecidability are robustly dead; the rest are dormant (method-relative) | SUPPORTED | a permanently-dead empirical question no method could ever revive |

### The headline: "No Question → No Knowledge"?

Two readings, the now-familiar split (exactly paralleling Warrant's C1/C2):

- **Reading A — inquired/discursive knowledge (SUPPORTED).**
  > **Theorem NQ1.** Knowledge produced *by inquiry* is intrinsically an answer to a
  > question; a proposition detached from the question it answers is (Collingwood)
  > not fully meaningful and not *inquired* knowledge. So: **no question → no inquired
  > knowledge.** SUPPORTED. *Proof-sketch:* inquiry's output is, by definition, the
  > resolution of a question's partition; with no question there is no partition to
  > resolve, hence no discursive result. *Falsifier:* discursive knowledge that
  > answers no question, explicit or implicit — none is credibly on offer.
- **Reading B — ALL knowledge, including immediate (CONTESTED).**
  > "*Every* piece of knowledge, even perceptual/a-priori, requires a prior question."
  > CONTESTED: perception seems to deliver knowledge un-asked ("I just see the tree").
  > *For:* theory-ladenness of observation (Hanson, Kuhn) — perception is already
  > question-shaped by the concepts brought to it. *Against:* foundationalist immediate
  > knowledge is non-erotetic. No case-proof settles it. CONTESTED.

**Recovered synthesis (the precise theorem):** *No question → no inquired knowledge*
is SUPPORTED; *no question → no knowledge whatsoever* is CONTESTED (the theory-ladenness
debate). The strong operational reading survives for any *active* knower: **whatever a
system comes to know by seeking, it knows only as the answer to a question it was able
to pose** — so the *ceiling on what an inquiring system can discover is set by the
questions it can form** (file 00, I4: you can only ask what you know enough to frame).
LIKELY, and it is the load-bearing result: an inquirer's reach is bounded, above, by
its question-forming capacity — not by its answering capacity.

This is the exact dual of Warrant's load-bearing result. Warrant: *the licence to
assert-as-known is bounded above by the warrant possessed.* Inquiry: *the reach of
discovery is bounded above by the questions formable.* Together they bracket knowledge
from both ends — what you may claim, and what you can even seek.
