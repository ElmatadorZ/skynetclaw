## Red Team

### Attack 1 — "Decision = Commitment" is just wrong (the strongest attack on the thesis)
A commitment can exist with no decision (a habit, a standing intention you never decided
on), and a decision can be made with no lasting commitment (a snap call you forget the
instant it is executed). So the identity fails both ways. **Verdict: DOWNGRADED — and the
volume already conceded it (DT2).** The bare identity *is* false (act ≠ status); the
defensible claim is DT2′: **deciding is the act that constitutively confers commitment.**
On the two alleged counterexamples: a *habit* is precisely a commitment that has *lost its
origin in a decision* — which is why we call it habitual rather than decided (it has
decayed toward standing disposition, Vol II preference); and a *snap call you truly retain
no commitment to* is, on inspection, a *reaction*, not a decision (nothing was settled to
be forgotten). **Residue:** the identity is a category error; the constitutive relation
(DT2′) survives both counterexamples, which turn out to be the *boundary cases that
locate* the act/status distinction rather than breaking it.

### Attack 2 — This is analytic: you defined decision as "commitment-forming" then proved it needs commitment
DT1 (No Commitment → No Decision) is true by the file-00 definition — circular and empty
(the fourth time this objection lands: NQ1, T1, VT1, now DT1). **Verdict: PARTIAL (as
before, and the recurrence is itself evidence).** The headline is near-analytic; the
*substantive* content is elsewhere: (i) that the commitment is a **distinct, separable
stage** (proven non-analytically by akrasia — a decision whose commitment outlives and
diverges from action); (ii) that its quality criterion is **warrant, not outcome** (Q6, a
falsifiable and non-obvious claim resisting the intuitive "judge by results"); and (iii)
the **Commitment ≅ Warrant** symmetry (DT3), which is a discovery, not a definition.
**Residue:** credit the separable-stage result, the warrant-quality result, and the
symmetry — not the near-tautological headline. That *this exact objection recurs across
all five No-X→No-Y theorems* is a datapoint for the meta-claim (VT2/DT3): the stack is
self-similar down to its objections.

### Attack 3 — You smuggled Expected Utility back in through "warrant"
Q6 says decision quality = warrant = "calibrated beliefs + coherent value + sound
combination" — which *is* the EU apparatus you claimed not to presuppose. **Verdict:
SURVIVES, with a real distinction.** Warrant-quality is *framework-neutral*: a
boundedly-rational satisficer can be *warranted* (it deliberated as well as its situation
and resources permitted — ecological rationality, Gigerenzer) without computing any
expected utility. Warrant grades *whether the agent was entitled to close where it did*,
which EU-optimality is one *idealized* sufficient condition for, not the definition of.
**Residue:** EU is a *model* of warranted decision in the risk×commensurable interior
(DT4), not the meaning of decision quality; a warranted decision need not be an EU-maximal
one. The volume uses "warrant" precisely to *avoid* making EU constitutive — the opposite
of smuggling it in.

### Attack 4 — The ontology is single-agent and ignores time
Real decisions are strategic (other agents) and temporal (sequences, revision), which the
static single-shot ontology omits. **Verdict: DOWNGRADED (scope, owned).** Strategic
interaction is explicitly deferred (Prisoner's Dilemma → Vol VII Governance); temporal
extension is explicitly the subject of Vol IV (Planning = commitment extended over time),
which DT3's mirror (Planning ≅ Inquiry) is designed to receive. **Residue:** Vol III is
the *single, present-tense* decision ontology on purpose — the atom Planning and
Governance compose. Claiming otherwise would over-reach; the scope is drawn where the next
volumes begin.

### Attack 5 — Warrant-quality collapses into outcome-quality over time
If a decision process *systematically* produces bad outcomes, we *revise* our judgment of
its warrant — so warrant is just long-run outcome after all. **Verdict: SURVIVES,
sharpened.** This conflates two roles of outcomes (Vol III·03): outcomes are *illegitimate*
as the criterion of a *single* decision (resulting), but *legitimate* as **feedback that
updates the belief and value inputs** for *future* decisions (learning, Vol VI). A run of
bad outcomes rationally lowers our estimate of a process's warrant *by updating what the
agent should have believed* — it does not make the *original* decision retroactively
unwarranted given the *then-available* evidence. **Residue:** outcome is feedback into
warrant's *inputs* across time, never the measure of warrant at a *time*; `outcome :
decision :: truth : belief` holds — truth updates future beliefs without making a past
warranted belief retroactively unwarranted. The distinction is exactly the one Part I drew
for warrant vs truth, transferred intact.

### Attack 6 — Self-application: was writing this volume a decision?
Turn it on itself. Writing Vol III was a decision: options (frame as "how to decide" vs
"what is a decision"), a value (fidelity + the mission's constraint), a belief (the
recovered literature), a **closure** (committing to the ontology-first frame and the
Commitment thesis), and a **commitment** (these six files, reasoned *from* thereafter).
Its quality is claimable only as **warrant** — the frame was entitled by the mission and
the literature — *not* as outcome (whether the thesis ultimately holds). And DT3 was
*tested*, not assumed (Attack 1 run against it). **Verdict: SURVIVES.** A theory of
decision whose own construction graded itself by anticipated outcome rather than warrant,
or asserted its thesis rather than testing it, would violate its own Q6 and DT2′; this one
closes at warrant and commits defeasibly. LIKELY.

### Attack 7 — Commitment ≅ Warrant is a pleasing analogy, not a result
The four-row mirror is aesthetic pattern-matching; analogies are cheap. **Verdict:
SURVIVES — because it earns its keep predictively, twice.** (i) It *predicted* the quality
criterion: if Commitment ≅ Warrant, decision quality should be warrant-like (process/
entitlement), *not* outcome-like — and decision analysis's independent *resulting* result
confirms exactly that (Q6). An analogy that predicts a non-obvious, independently-attested
fact is a structural claim. (ii) It *predicts the shape of Vol IV*: Planning ≅ Inquiry
(extend the status over a structured field with no new axioms) — a testable claim the next
volume will confirm or break. **Residue:** the symmetry is LIKELY→SUPPORTED as *structure*
(load-bearing, predictive), with individual rows defeasible; it is credited as a working
theoretical claim, not decoration, precisely because it has already paid predictive rent.

## D10 · Summary — what Volume III delivers

**The answer to "what is a decision?" (SUPPORTED):** a decision is *the act that closes
deliberation by forming a commitment* — the unique operator that consumes a **belief**
(mind→world, Part I) and a **value** (world→mind, Vol II) and emits a **commitment**
(world→mind, settled). It adds no new object to the world; it adds one *act* (Decision)
and one *status* (Commitment). Its differentia against mere choice is the commitment; its
quality is *warrant*, not outcome; its closure is triggered by Inquiry's stopping rule;
and it is well-posed only in the interior where both its inputs are tame.

**The load-bearing results:**
1. **The Commitment thesis, tested and refined.** *No Commitment → No Decision* is true
   (DT1). *Decision = Commitment* is a category error corrected to *deciding is the act
   that confers commitment* (DT2′). Akrasia proves the commitment is a **separable stage**.
2. **Commitment ≅ Warrant — the completed symmetry** (DT3). Commitment is the practical
   twin of Warrant: a defeasible normative status licensing the inner→outer crossing.
   Confirmed twice — in the ontology (bridge role) and in the quality criterion (decision
   quality = practical warrant, not outcome, mirroring belief quality = warrant, not
   truth). This is the mission's deep hypothesis, and it *survives* — sharpened only by
   distinguishing the licensing *act* (Deciding ≅ Warranting) from the licensed *status*
   (Commitment ≅ Warrant).
3. **Decision is the second bridge.** The hinge doc named Observation the shared *input*
   node; Vol III names Decision the shared *output* node — belief flows in from Knowing,
   combines with value, commits out to Acting. The stack's figure-eight crosses itself
   twice, and both crossings now have theories.
4. **Two theorems import Part I with no new axiom** — the closure rule *is* Inquiry's
   stopping rule (DT5), and Newcomb shows the belief-input carries Part I's causation
   whole. Part II is not rebuilding Part I; it is *reusing* it under fit-reversal.
5. **The double frontier** (DT4) — decision inherits *both* silent frontiers (belief
   ignorance + value incommensurability); EU occupies the tame interior, provably partial.

**How it sits in the stack.** Vol II handed Decision a defined primitive; Vol III consumes
it and returns a defined *status* — Commitment — that Vol IV (Planning) will extend over
time exactly as Inquiry extends Warrant, and Vol VII (Governance) will regulate across
agents. The four-row mirror (primitive / licensing act / status / output, + expression, +
forward extension) is now complete on both sides, which is the structural evidence that
Part II is not a sequel to Part I but its **reflection under direction-of-fit reversal**.

**What Volume III does NOT do (scope, stated so it is not overclaimed):** it is *single-
agent* (strategic interaction → Vol VII) and *single-shot* (temporal sequencing → Vol IV);
it does not resolve CDT vs EDT, does not ground the *authority* of commitment, and does not
prescribe *how* to decide beyond locating EU/Bayes/RL as the tame interior of a doubly-
bounded space. It answers *what a decision is*, hands Planning a status to extend, and
leaves *how to decide well* to be filled inside the boundary it drew.

**One-line result:** *No Commitment → No Decision* — จริง; **Decision = Commitment** —
almost: deciding is the *act* that confers commitment, and **Commitment is the Warrant of
the Acting stack** — the defeasible licence that lets an inner value cross into an outer
act, graded like all warrant by entitlement, not by luck.
