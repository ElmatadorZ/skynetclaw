## The central hypothesis, tested (Q10)

The mission proposes two forms and asks *is it true?* — they must be separated, because
they have different truth-values.

### DT1 · No Commitment → No Decision — **SUPPORTED**
**Statement.** An act that forms no commitment — settles nothing, binds nothing, leaves
every option as open after as before — is not a decision; it is continued deliberation
or a mere pick.

**Support: SUPPORTED for genuine/deliberative decision; the strong "represented
commitment" form CONTESTED — the same double structure as C1, NQ1, T1, VT1.**
- *Constitutive reading:* commitment is the **differentia** of decision (Vol III·00 Q4,
  axiom D4). Choice ⊋ decision, and what a decision adds to a choice is precisely the
  commitment. Remove it and you have a Buridan pick, not a decision. Follows analytically
  from the ontology.
- *Deflationary perimeter:* a thermostat "commits" only in the as-if sense (an embodied,
  unrepresented settling). Whether *represented* commitment is required for *genuine*
  decision is the recurring thermostat cut (Vol I·02) — CONTESTED, unresolved.

**Verdict on "จริงไหม": Yes.** No commitment → no decision is true, and non-trivially so:
it has the content that the defining work of a decision is the *binding*, not the
selection — which is what Buridan (commitment created where value is flat) and Akrasia
(commitment intact while action fails) independently confirm. **Falsifier:** a genuine
decision that produces no commitment of any kind (settles and binds nothing) — the
Buridan pick is the standing test, and it is a *non*-decision, so it confirms rather than
refutes.

### DT2 · Decision = Commitment — **DOWNGRADED (a category correction)**
**Statement (as proposed).** A decision *is* a commitment.

**Verdict: DOWNGRADED — true in extension, false in category.** The identity conflates an
**act** with a **status**:
- **Decision** is the *act* — the event that closes deliberation.
- **Commitment** is the *status* — the settled, binding intention the act produces.

They are **extensionally inseparable** (there is no decision without a commitment made,
and no commitment without a decision that made it — DT1 gives one direction, and a
commitment that arose from no deciding is a mere standing disposition/preference, not a
commitment, giving the other) but **categorially distinct** (an occurrence vs an enduring
normative state). So the precise theorem is:

> **DT2′ · Decision is the act that constitutively confers Commitment.** Deciding *is*
> committing-as-an-act; the commitment *is* what one has thereby. Identity of *process*,
> distinction of *category*.

This is exactly how Part I treats its own pair: *judging* (the act) confers *warrant*
(the status); one does not say "judgment = warrant," one says "judging is warranting, and
warrant is what one thereby has." The mission's `Decision = Commitment` is the same
near-identity, and it earns the same careful correction. **Falsifier:** a decision whose
commitment can be fully identified with the *act itself* with no residual enduring status
(no future-guiding, no premise-to-reason-from) — akrasia (a persisting commitment
outlasting and diverging from action) refutes this.

### DT3 · Commitment ≅ Warrant — the completed symmetry (**SUPPORTED**)
**The mission's deep hypothesis: Commitment has the same status as Warrant — a bridge
between inner and outer.** *This survives, and is the volume's principal result.*

Commitment and Warrant are both **defeasible normative statuses that license the crossing
from an inner state to an outer expression:**
- **Warrant** licenses *assertion* from *belief*: with warrant, a belief may be
  asserted-as-known (Knowledge); the licence is defeasible (new evidence revokes it).
- **Commitment** licenses *action* from *value*: with a commitment, a value may be
  acted-on-as-settled; the licence is defeasible (new evidence/value revokes it).

Both are *entitlements*, not mere psychological states (warrant = epistemic entitlement;
commitment = practical bindingness); both are *defeasible*; both are the *third term* that
closes their triad and stands ready at the inner/outer boundary. Confirmed **twice
independently**: structurally (the ontology, Vol III·00 — Commitment sits where the
licensed output sits) and evaluatively (the quality criterion, Vol III·03 Q6 — decision
quality *is* practical warrant, not outcome, exactly as belief quality is warrant not
truth).

**The completed four-row mirror (SUPPORTED as structure; individual rows LIKELY):**

| role | Knowing | Acting |
|---|---|---|
| **primitive** (raw input) | Belief | Value |
| **licensing act** | Judging / warranting | **Deciding** |
| **normative status** (the bridge) | **Warrant** | **Commitment** |
| **licensed output** | Knowledge (= warranted belief) | the committed intention (→ Policy) |
| **outer expression** | Assertion | Action |
| **forward extension** | Inquiry | Planning |

So the precise correspondences the volume establishes: **Deciding ≅ Warranting** (the
act), **Commitment ≅ Warrant** (the status — the mission's hypothesis, confirmed),
**Action ≅ Assertion** (the expression), and, previewing Vol IV, **Planning ≅ Inquiry**
(the forward extension of the status over a structured field). The user's prediction —
that Vol IV becomes "extending Commitment into a sequence of actions" with no new axioms —
is thereby underwritten: Planning extends Commitment as Inquiry extends Warrant.
**Falsifier:** show Commitment lacks a defeasible-entitlement structure, or that decision
quality is *not* warrant-like (is genuinely outcome-measured) — either would break the
mirror.

### DT4 · The Double-Frontier Theorem
**Statement.** A decision consumes a belief input and a value input, so it is well-posed
only where *both* are tame; it goes **silent** where *either* frontier is hit — belief
*ignorance* (unconceived outcomes, Q5) or value *incommensurability* (Vol II Q8).
**Support: SUPPORTED.** **Consequence:** EU/Bayesian decision theory occupies the
*risk × commensurable* interior — powerful and provably partial. Decision is doubly
bounded, once from each half of the stack it bridges — the most complete inheritance of
the stack's recurring "strong in the middle, silent at the frontier." **Falsifier:** a
procedure handling genuine ignorance *and* genuine incommensurability without covertly
taming one.

### DT5 · The Closure-Rule Theorem
**Statement.** The rule that *ends* a decision's deliberation is Inquiry's *stopping
rule*: close and commit when the marginal value of further deliberation falls below its
cost. **Support: SUPPORTED** (constructive: DF2/DF3 are its two mis-firings, Vol III·03).
**Consequence:** Part I's forward theory supplies Part II's closure criterion with **no
new axiom** — Inquiry-stopping and Decision-closure are one theorem seen from the two
directions of fit. **Falsifier:** optimal closure provably diverges from optimal
inquiry-stopping.

## Q9 · Open problems
1. **The bindingness of commitment.** Why does a past decision *bind* present-you —
   what grounds the authority of a prior commitment over a later self who could
   reconsider (Bratman's stability has a *function*, but its *normative force* is
   ungrounded)? This is the rootless-root residue at the decision layer: the *authority*
   of commitment, like the authority of terminal value (Vol II op1) and the ultimate
   ground of warrant, is OPEN — and plausibly the *same* hole. OPEN.
2. **CDT vs EDT (Newcomb).** Whether the belief-input's causal reading should be
   interventional (`do`) or evidential (`see`) is unresolved; it is a Part-I causation
   dispute surfacing in Part II. OPEN.
3. **Multi-agent decision.** "The rational decision" is not well-defined when the
   environment contains other deciders (Prisoner's Dilemma); equilibrium concepts are
   plural. Deferred to Governance (Vol VII). OPEN (scoped out).
4. **Bounded rationality's norm.** Satisficing is what decision *is* (Vol III·02); a
   complete theory of *good* satisficing (which aspiration levels, which heuristics when —
   Gigerenzer's ecological rationality) is unfinished. OPEN.
5. **Decision under ignorance.** The belief-frontier (unconceived outcomes) has no
   framework; deciding inside a possibly-incomplete frame is unavoidable and
   uncertifiable — the dual of Inquiry's unconceived alternatives. OPEN.
6. **Represented vs as-if commitment.** The thermostat cut (does genuine decision require
   *represented* commitment?) recurs here as it did in Vols I–II. OPEN.

## Settled vs open (honest ledger)
- **Settled (SUPPORTED):** decision = closure + commitment (D1–D4); No Commitment → No
  Decision (DT1); Decision-is-the-act-conferring-Commitment (DT2′); Commitment ≅ Warrant
  (DT3, twice-confirmed); quality = warrant not outcome; the double frontier (DT4); the
  closure rule = inquiry stopping rule (DT5).
- **Settled (LIKELY):** the individual rows of the four-row mirror; Planning ≅ Inquiry
  (previewed, Vol IV to discharge).
- **Open:** bindingness of commitment (1, the rootless root here); CDT/EDT (2); multi-agent
  (3); good satisficing (4); ignorance (5); represented-vs-as-if commitment (6).

The pattern holds a fifth time: **determinate in the middle, open at the foundation.**
The decision layer's foundation-hole is the *authority of commitment* — why a settling
binds — and it is very likely the *same* hole as terminal-value's authority and warrant's
ground, now seen at the act/status layer. The stack's open bottoms keep converging on one
question: *what licenses the crossing from inner to outer* — and Vol III's contribution is
to show that on the Acting side that licence has a name (Commitment), a twin (Warrant),
and a quality criterion (practical warrant) — everything except a ground.
