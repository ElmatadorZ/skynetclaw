## Q6 · Decision quality — outcome, process, or warrant?

The decisive recovered distinction (decision analysis: Howard; Raiffa; popularized by
Annie Duke as *resulting*):

**A good decision is not a good outcome.** You can decide well and lose (good process,
bad luck) or decide terribly and win (bad process, good luck). Outcomes are jointly
produced by the decision *and* the world's chance; grading the decision by the outcome
confounds the two. So:

- **Outcome-based quality — REJECTED as the measure.** Hindsight- and luck-contaminated;
  it grades the *world's cooperation*, not the *agent's act*. Useful as *feedback*
  (learning, Vol VI), useless as the *criterion of the decision itself*. SUPPORTED
  (rejection).
- **Process-based quality — NECESSARY.** Were the options well-framed, the beliefs
  calibrated, the value coherent, the combination sound? This grades the act, not the
  luck. SUPPORTED.
- **Warrant-based quality — the deep answer (SUPPORTED, and the volume's second spine).**
  A decision is good *iff it was warranted by the agent's evidence and value at
  decision-time* — iff, given what the agent was entitled to believe (Part I warrant) and
  what it valued (Vol II), the commitment was the entitled one. **Decision quality is
  practical warrant.**

**The structural payoff (SUPPORTED):** this is *exactly* the shape of epistemic warrant.
In Part I, a belief's quality = its **warrant**, *not* its **truth** (a warranted false
belief is epistemically blameless; a lucky true guess is not knowledge). In Vol III, a
decision's quality = its **warrant**, *not* its **outcome** (a warranted unlucky decision
is blameless; a reckless lucky one is not skill). The mapping is precise:

| Knowing | Acting |
|---|---|
| belief's *target* = truth | decision's *target* = good outcome |
| belief's *quality* = warrant (not truth) | decision's *quality* = warrant (not outcome) |
| lucky true guess ≠ knowledge | reckless lucky win ≠ good decision |
| warranted false belief = blameless | warranted unlucky loss = blameless |

`outcome : decision :: truth : belief` — the aimed-at, but *not* the measure of the act.
This independently confirms the Commitment ≅ Warrant symmetry (Q10) **from the quality
side**: the thing that grades a commitment is warrant, the very currency that grades a
belief. The symmetry appears twice — in the ontology (the bridge role) and in the quality
criterion — which is strong evidence it is real, not imposed. SUPPORTED.

## Q8 / D4 · Failure taxonomy — deficits of closure-timing and input integrity

**The organizing principle (SUPPORTED):** a decision is `close-and-commit at the right
warrant`. Its failures are therefore of two structural kinds — **mis-timed closure**
(closing too late or too early relative to warrant) and **corrupted inputs** (bad belief
or bad value flowing in). Each localizes on the decision graph (file 01).

| # | Failure | Structural fault (graph) | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| DF1 | **Indecision** | closure (D3) never fires; field stays open | mis-timed: too late (∞) |
| DF2 | **Evidence paralysis** | the inquiry *stopping rule* never triggers; endless belief-gathering | mis-timed: too late |
| DF3 | **Premature decision** | closure fires *before* adequate warrant | mis-timed: too early |
| DF4 | **Reward domination** | the **Reward** sensor (Vol II) overrides **Value** in the combination | corrupted value-input |
| DF5 | **Preference reversal** | the **Value** ordering flips under framing (Kahneman–Tversky) | unstable value-input |

### DF1 · Indecision — *closure never fires*
The decision act (D3) fails to execute; deliberation runs without terminating. The agent
has options, beliefs, and value but never *commits*. This is the pure failure of the
differentia — everything present except the one thing that makes a decision a decision.
SUPPORTED.

### DF2 · Evidence paralysis — *the stopping rule never triggers*
A special, seductive form of DF1: the agent keeps *gathering evidence* (running Inquiry)
to postpone committing — "analysis paralysis." The fault is precisely that **Inquiry's
stopping theorem** (from Part I) never fires: the marginal value of more deliberation is
treated as always positive, so closure is deferred forever. **Decision inherits Inquiry's
stopping rule as its closure rule** — you stop inquiring and commit when the marginal
value of more information falls below its cost. DF2 is that rule left unexecuted. This is
a direct, load-bearing bridge from Part I: *the same stopping theorem that ends inquiry
begins decision.* SUPPORTED.

### DF3 · Premature decision — *committing beyond warrant*
The mirror of DF2: closure fires *before* the agent is warranted — deciding on
insufficient evidence/deliberation. **This is the practical form of C1's overclaim**:
where C1 forbids *asserting-as-known* beyond warrant, DF3 is *committing-as-settled*
beyond warrant — recklessness (the hinge doc's Part-II overreach). The runtime overclaim
detector (the CEE bridge) guards the C1 side; DF3 is its Acting-side twin, and a future
"commitment-warrant" check would be its analog. SUPPORTED, and it ties the failure
taxonomy back to the built bridge.

### DF4 · Reward domination — *the sensor overrides the criterion*
The Reward signal (Vol II's fallible value-sensor) hijacks the decision, overriding the
endorsed Value — impulsivity, temptation, hyperbolic discounting. Structurally the
Value→Decision edge is captured by the Reward branch. This is the decision-stage
appearance of Vol II's reward≠value: the sensor wins the combination it was only meant to
inform. SUPPORTED.

### DF5 · Preference reversal — *the criterion won't hold still*
The value ordering flips with framing/description (Kahneman–Tversky; Lichtenstein–Slovic)
— the same options ranked differently under "gain" vs "loss" framing. If the criterion is
unstable, **the commitment cannot stabilize** (D4 fails to bind, because what it binds to
keeps changing). This is Vol II's downgraded-cascade (Attack 4, constructed/framed value)
striking at the decision stage. SUPPORTED.

## The master axis (SUPPORTED)
DF1–DF3 array on a single axis — **timing of closure relative to warrant**:

```
   too late  ◀─────────────── RIGHT ───────────────▶  too early
   Indecision / Evidence         close-and-commit         Premature
   paralysis (DF1,DF2)          at sufficient warrant     decision (DF3)
```

The healthy decision closes *at* the warrant the situation affords — Inquiry's stopping
rule as the closure trigger. DF4–DF5 are orthogonal: they corrupt the *value input* so
that even well-timed closure commits to the wrong thing. **Two failure dimensions:
*when* you close (timing vs warrant) and *what* you close on (value integrity)** — and
they compose with Vol II's value-failures (DF4/DF5 are the decision-stage faces of VF-
reward-hacking and VF-framing). SUPPORTED.

## What the taxonomy establishes
1. **Quality is warrant, not outcome** — and every failure is a departure from
   warrant-appropriate closure or an input corruption; the taxonomy is bounded by the
   graph. SUPPORTED.
2. **Decision's closure rule *is* Inquiry's stopping rule** — DF2/DF3 are its two
   mis-firings. Part I's forward theory (Inquiry) supplies Part II's closure criterion
   with no new axiom — the continuity the whole roadmap predicts. SUPPORTED.
3. **DF3 is the Acting-side C1 overclaim** — recklessness = committing beyond warrant —
   which is why the CEE bridge (built) and a future commitment-warrant check are the same
   instrument pointed at the two directions of fit. SUPPORTED.

## Falsifiers
The "quality = warrant not outcome" thesis fails if a decision with impeccable
warrant-at-time is correctly judged *bad* solely because it lost (pure resulting is
correct) — decision analysis's standing result resists this. The timing-axis claim fails
if a failure is exhibited that is neither mis-timed closure nor input corruption. The
"closure rule = Inquiry stopping rule" claim fails if optimal decision-closure provably
diverges from optimal inquiry-stopping.
