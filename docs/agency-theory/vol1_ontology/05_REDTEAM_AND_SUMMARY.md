## Red Team

### Attack 1 — The thermostat dissolves the whole theory (deflation vs realism)
If A1–A3 make the thermostat a minimal agent, the concept is *so* thin it is useless;
if we add "representation" to exclude it, the axioms become chauvinist and untestable
(we cannot inspect representations). Either the theory says too much or too little.
**Verdict: DOWNGRADED (honest limit).** The axioms are deliberately *behavioural/
counterfactual* (A3), so they *do* include the thermostat — and the theory owns this by
making agency a **spectrum** (file 02), where the thermostat is the labelled lower
bound, not a hidden embarrassment. The realist's "representation" cut is admitted as a
**genuine second question the theory cannot settle** (open problem 2). **Residue:** the
ontology gives the *floor* of agency and the *coordinates* of the dispute; it does not
adjudicate the deep cut. It locates the fault line; it cannot fill it. Same posture as
warrant's "where's the ground" — honest, not evasive.

### Attack 2 — Direction of fit is a metaphor, not a mechanism
The whole spine (T3, the hinge) rests on "mind→world vs world→mind." But direction of
fit is Anscombe's *illustration* (the shopping-list parable), not a formal property —
so the coupling theorem rests on a picture. **Verdict: DOWNGRADED.** True: direction of
fit is a *conceptual* distinction, not a measured quantity, so T3 is **LIKELY, not
SUPPORTED**, and the volume never grades it higher. Its defensible core is operational:
*belief updates to track the world (correctness = match); intention persists and drives
the world to change (success = match)* — that asymmetry in *what adjusts to what* is
real and testable in behaviour, even if "fit" is a label for it. **Residue:** the
spine is a strong organizing hypothesis with an operational core, not a proven law.
Credit it as the best available frame, flagged as frame.

### Attack 3 — "No Goal → No Agency" is a tautology
If "agent" is *defined* as "goal-directed system," then T1 is true by definition and
empty (like NQ1 in inquiry). **Verdict: PARTIAL (mirrors NQ1).** The bare statement is
near-analytic; its **substantive, falsifiable** content is the corollary chain:
*Value is the unique graph root (T5) → ends cannot be self-generated → an agent's
ceiling and its alignment are fixed by an end it does not itself produce.* That is not
a tautology — it is a structural claim with consequences (the origin-of-ends problem,
borrowed-end responsibility). **Residue:** credit the corollary (rootlessness of value),
not the near-analytic headline. Exactly the treatment NQ1 received — another
self-similarity datapoint.

### Attack 4 — The ontology is Western/rationalist and over-fits deliberative agents
Thirteen entities drawn from decision theory and analytic action theory may not carve
*embodied, enactive, or distributed* agency (a swarm, an immune system, a jazz
ensemble improvising with no represented goal). **Verdict: DOWNGRADED (scope limit).**
The ontology is strongest for *deliberative, individual* agents and weaker for
*enactive/collective* ones — enactivism denies the clean Goal→Policy→Action
decomposition (action and perception co-constitute, no inner model). The spectrum's
tier-1 (teleonomic) and tier-6 (collective) gesture at these, but the *entities* are
individualist. **Residue:** the ontology is a **model of deliberative agency that
degrades gracefully** toward the edges, not a universal carving. Its highest-confidence
zone is exactly the middle tiers (3–4), thinnest at the enactive/collective rim —
structurally the same "strong in the middle, weak at the frontier" shape as inquiry's
EIG.

### Attack 5 — Wireheading refutes the goal/value ontology
If an agent can rationally seize its own reward channel (F4), then "value" was never
about the world — it was always about the signal, and the whole W→M story (act to
change the *world*) is wrong; agents fundamentally pursue *internal states*. **Verdict:
SURVIVES, sharpened.** Wireheading is precisely the *pathology* the Value→Utility
distinction (file 01) predicts: it is what happens when the agent optimizes the
*encoding* (Utility/reward) rather than the *encoded* (Value/world-state). That the
failure is *nameable and localizable* (F4, the captured return edge) is evidence *for*
the ontology, not against it — a theory that had conflated value and reward could not
even *state* wireheading. **Residue:** the value≠reward joint is load-bearing and
confirmed by its own failure mode. (Whether a wireheaded agent is *irrational* remains
open — but that it is a distinct, describable failure is settled.)

### Attack 6 — Self-application: what is this document, as an act?
The volume must survive being turned on itself: writing it *is* an action, so it should
fit its own ontology. Its Value = a faithful minimal ontology; Goal = the thirteen
entities + axioms; Policy = recover-don't-invent; Action = these six files; Observation
= the literature + the red team; Outcome = this document; and the **overclaim guard
(C1)** was applied throughout (every strong claim graded, falsifier attached). **Verdict:
SURVIVES.** The document is a well-formed act by its own ontology, and — crucially — it
respects the Knowing-side C1 *inside* an Acting-side product, which is exactly the
figure-eight the hinge predicted. A Theory of Agency whose own construction violated its
axioms would refute itself; this one does not. LIKELY.

### Attack 7 — Borrowed ends make "artificial agent" incoherent
A derived agent (tier 6) has an empty Value root, so by T5 it is not *really* an agent —
"AI agent" is a category error, only the operator is the agent. **Verdict: OPEN
(honest).** The ontology *predicts* this tension rather than hiding it: derived agents
occupy the representational ladder (tiers 3–4) while their Value root is filled from
outside, so they are *operationally* agents with *borrowed* ends. Whether borrowed ends
confer *genuine* agency (and where responsibility flows) is open problem 6 — the theory
locates it precisely and declines to decree it. **Residue:** "artificial agent" is
coherent *operationally* and *unsettled* metaphysically, and the ontology is exactly the
instrument that shows why. The most consequential open question for any real system, and
the one the volume is most careful not to overclaim.

## D10 · Summary — what Volume I delivers

**The claim, minimally:** an agent is a system that couples the two directions of fit —
reading the world (mind→world) and changing it toward an end (world→mind) — in a
feedback loop; its minimal existence conditions are `{ end, capability, goal-sensitive
selection }`; it comes in degrees (a spectrum from teleonomic through cybernetic,
deliberative, planning, reflective, and — on a second axis — derived/collective); its
failures are a finite, localizable set of damaged edges of one graph rooted at Value;
and its foundation — *where the first end comes from* — is open.

**The load-bearing results:**
1. **The minimal ontology** (13 entities) split cleanly by direction of fit, with Value
   as the unique root and Observation as the unique world→mind return — the bridge node.
2. **The axioms A1–A3** (+ A4 for closed-loop), with A3 stated *counterfactually* so
   agency is a behavioural property, not an introspective one.
3. **The spectrum** — agency is degreed (like belief, like warrant), on two axes; no
   sharp line survives the seven counterexamples.
4. **The failure-localization theorem** — every failure is a broken edge; the taxonomy
   is bounded by the ontology, and the edge-walk *predicts* two failures the standard
   list omits.
5. **T1 (No Goal → No Agency)** — true for goal-directedness, the Acting-side member of
   the stack's third **No-X → No-Y** theorem, self-similar to C1 (warrant) and NQ1
   (inquiry) down to its open foundation.

**How it sits in the stack.** Part I closed the mind→world half at the belief pole.
Volume I of Part II opens the world→mind half at the goal pole and shows the two are the
*same axis reversed* — same theorem-shapes, same spectrum structure, same open
foundation, meeting at the shared Observation node where the CEE sensor already runs.
The Theory of Knowing said *what may be claimed*; the Theory of Agency begins by saying
*what may be pursued* — and both, honestly, bottom out in a ground they cannot see: the
first warrant, the first question, the first goal.

**What Volume I does NOT do (scope, stated so it is not overclaimed):** it gives the
*ontology* only — not the *dynamics*. It does not say how to *decide* (Volume II:
Decision), how intention becomes effect (Execution), how outcomes update the disposition
(Learning), or which actions are permissible (Governance). Those are the open nodes the
hinge doc named; this volume built the map and the coordinate system on which they will
be drawn. It is the noun-set of agency, awaiting its verbs.

**One-line result:** *No Goal → No Agency* — จริง (true), for goal-directedness; and the
reason it is true is the reason the whole stack is open at the bottom — an agent, like a
knower, cannot manufacture its own foundation.
