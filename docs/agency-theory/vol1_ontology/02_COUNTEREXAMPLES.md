## The recovered result: agency is a spectrum, not a predicate

Like *belief* and *warrant* in Part I, **agent** is a cluster concept with degrees, not
a classical category with a sharp boundary. The cases sort into six tiers of a single
ladder (SUPPORTED as a taxonomy; the tier *cuts* are LIKELY):

1. **Teleonomic / as-if** (plant, virus-as-replicator) — goal-*directedness* installed
   by natural selection (Mayr's *teleonomy*), with no goal *held* by the system. The
   "purpose" is in the design history, not the present state. The intentional stance
   (Dennett) predicts them cheaply, but there is no inner end. *Below* the agency line
   for the realist; the *thinnest* as-if agency for the deflationist.
2. **Minimal cybernetic** (thermostat) — a *present* setpoint + feedback + goal-sensitive
   action (A1–A4), but no representation and no deliberation. McCarthy's provocation
   ("the thermostat believes it is too cold") lives exactly here. This is the **lower
   bound of agency** under the deflationary axioms — and the sharpest test of whether
   representation is required.
3. **Representational / deliberative** (a robot or animal with a world-model) — selects
   actions by *modelling* outcomes against a *represented* goal. Crosses the realist's
   line: the goal is now encoded and reasoned over.
4. **Planning agency** (Bratman) — forms and executes **intentions** and **plans**,
   holds **commitments** stable against reconsideration, achieving temporal coherence
   and coordination across time. Adds the Commitment/Intention nodes of the ontology.
5. **Reflective / personal agency** (Frankfurt) — has **second-order volitions**
   (values about which desires to be moved by); the will can endorse or repudiate its
   own first-order desires. This is *personhood*, a proper superset of agency.
6. **Derived / collective agency** (corporation, LLM-in-a-loop) — genuine agency whose
   ends and capabilities are *borrowed from* or *distributed across* other agents. Real
   (List & Pettit argue groups have genuine, non-reducible attitudes) but
   *non-fundamental*: switch off the constituting agents and it vanishes.

**The spectrum is orthogonal in one place:** derived agency (tier 6) can sit at *any*
representational height — a corporation can plan (tier 4) while being derived (tier 6).
So "derived vs intrinsic" is a **second axis** (origin of the end), crossing the
representational ladder. Two axes, not one line. LIKELY.

## Boundary cases dissected (D5)

- **Thermostat — the lower bound.** It passes A1–A4 by the *behavioural* (A3
  counterfactual) test: change the setpoint, the behaviour changes. The only thing it
  lacks is *representation*. So the thermostat is the precise place where the volume's
  contested cut bites: **if representation is necessary, the thermostat is not an agent;
  if the counterfactual A3 suffices, it is the smallest one.** The theory does not
  force the cut — it *locates* it, and reports honestly that the location is contested
  (red team, Attack 1).
- **LLM — the borrowed-end boundary.** A bare model has capability and can execute a
  goal-sensitive policy, but the *end is exogenous* (the prompt). It fails A1 in the
  *intrinsic* sense while passing it in the *borrowed* sense. An LLM *in an agent loop*
  (tools + a standing objective + memory) climbs to tier 3–4 **operationally**, yet the
  root Value node is still supplied from outside. This is why "is an LLM an agent?" has
  no yes/no answer: it is a **tier-6 (derived) system that can occupy tiers 3–4 of the
  representational ladder while its Value root stays empty.** SUPPORTED as a diagnosis;
  the moral status of borrowed ends is CONTESTED. *(This is also the honest mirror of
  the host system: an LLM-driven agent's ends are the operator's ends — the origin-of-
  ends problem is not academic for it.)*
- **Corporation — the collective boundary.** Distributes A1–A3 across members and
  procedures. The realism question (genuine agent vs legal fiction vs intentional-stance
  aggregate) is live; List–Pettit's group-agency result says a group *can* hold
  attitudes its members individually do not, making it non-reducible. CONTESTED, but the
  taxonomy places it cleanly as **tier-6 derived**, whatever its ultimate metaphysics.
- **Virus / Plant — the sub-agency boundary.** Both are goal-*directed* without a goal
  *held*: teleonomy, not teleology. They mark the **floor** below which "agency" is only
  a stance we adopt, with nothing inner answering to it. Placing them below tier 1
  (or as its thinnest edge) is the deflationist's honest lower limit.

## What the counterexamples prove about the ontology
1. **No sharp essence.** Every attempt to draw one line either excludes the thermostat
   (too high) or includes the virus (too low). Agency is degreed — the axioms A1–A4
   define the *minimal* rung, not a binary. SUPPORTED.
2. **Two independent axes.** Representational height (tiers 1→5) and origin of the end
   (intrinsic vs derived, the tier-6 axis). A full classification needs both. LIKELY.
3. **The contested add-on is representation, not rationality.** Every case that
   *feels* like a boundary (thermostat, LLM) turns on whether the goal is *represented*
   / *owned*, never on whether the agent is *rational*. This confirms Q2's ranking:
   representation is the deep cut; rationality is a norm, not a condition. SUPPORTED.

## Falsifiers
The spectrum is refuted by a single, principled, exceptionless line that correctly
sorts all seven cases into agent/non-agent with no residue (would restore a classical
category). The two-axis claim is refuted if origin-of-end and representational height
turn out to co-vary necessarily (no derived planner, no intrinsic reflex) — a
corporation that plans (derived + high) already strains this against the refuter.
