## Q1 · What is governance?

The traditions name one thing from different sides:
- **Reference monitor (Anderson).** The always-invoked, tamper-proof, verifiable mediator of
  every authority-bearing operation. Governance = *complete mediation of action against a
  policy*. SUPPORTED as the mechanism.
- **Separation of powers (Montesquieu).** Distribute authority so no locus is unchecked; the
  governor is *separated from* the governed. SUPPORTED as the structural condition.
- **Principal–agent (economics).** The principal's mechanism to align an autonomous agent's
  action with the principal's interest despite autonomy + information asymmetry. SUPPORTED as
  the alignment framing.
- **Secondary rules (Hart).** Law = primary rules (conduct) + *secondary* rules (rules about
  rules: recognition, change, adjudication). Governance is the secondary-rule layer. SUPPORTED.
- **Requisite variety (Ashby).** Only variety absorbs variety — a governor needs variety ≥
  the governed. SUPPORTED, and it *bounds* what governance can do (Vol VII·01).

**Recovered core (SUPPORTED):** *governance is the **second-order regulation of action** — a
layer that (i) holds a normative standard of which actions are permissible, (ii) mediates
every action against it completely, and (iii) enforces the bound — and that is **separated
from and superior to** the agent's own decision-to-act.* It is not more agency of the same
kind; it is **meta-agency: agency directed at an agent's agency.** Direction of fit: it is
**world→mind about the agent** — it makes the agent's *action-space* conform to a norm (a
second level of world→mind, one above the agent's own world→mind policy).

**Why it must exist (SUPPORTED, from Vol V):** the excess footprint means acting always
produces effects beyond any intention. Those effects are unauthored by the agent's own
commitment, so they cannot be governed by the agent's own decision — they require a regulator
*beyond* the agent. **Governance is the answer to "who is responsible for what no one
intended?"** Without it, the surplus of action is simply unowned.

## Q3 / D1 · The ontology of governance (eleven entities)

| Entity | Minimal definition | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Policy / Norm** | the standard of which actions are permissible | the governing content (W→M about actions) |
| **Authority** | justified power to permit/prohibit — *warrant for the governor* | Security-Theory link |
| **Mediation** | the checking of an action against the policy *before* it takes effect | Anderson; must be *complete* |
| **Permission / Prohibition** | the mediation verdict on a proposed action | ALLOW / DENY / ESCALATE |
| **Enforcement / Sanction** | the power to prevent or penalize non-compliant action | a norm with no teeth is advice |
| **Override / Veto** | the halt on irreversible/high-stakes action pending authority | the human gate; the excess-footprint guard |
| **Accountability** | the record of who did what, to whom the surplus is attributed | ownership of the footprint |
| **Legitimacy** | the *ground* of the governor's authority — why the bound is binding | **the open node** |
| **The Governed** | the agent whose action-space is bounded | Vol I–V |
| **The Governor** | the locus holding the policy + mediation + enforcement | separated from the governed |
| **The Commons** | the shared world/footprint multiple agents affect | Ostrom; multi-agent surplus |

## Q4 · Direction of fit — governance is the reflexive wrap

Governance is a **coupling**, like Decision (Vol III), Guidance (Vol V), and Learning (Vol
VI) — but it wraps the action node reflexively:
- it **observes** the *proposed* action (mind→world: sense what the agent is about to do),
- it **checks** it against a policy (a world→mind standard about actions), and
- it **permits/prohibits** (world→mind: constrains the agent's action-space).

So governance is the **fifth coupling** and the only *second-order* one: where Observation
sensed the world, Decision combined belief+value, Guidance steered the act, and Learning
revised from outcome, **Governance regulates the agent itself.** Its Part-I twin (from the
Knowing↔Acting mirror) is **Meta-Evaluation** — the reflexive normative layer that evaluates
the evaluating. `Governance : action :: Meta-Evaluation : belief-forming` — both are
normativity applied to the object process, not the object. SUPPORTED.

**The capstone consequence (LIKELY):** with Governance theorized, the return arc is complete
(`outcome → learn → govern → bounds next decide`), the figure-eight closes, and the Theory of
Agency is **complete as a system** — nine Knowing theories + seven Agency volumes joined at
the shared Observation node, with both confluences (forward: Decision; return: Learning) and
the reflexive wrap (Governance) all theorized.

## Falsifiers
Q1's core fails if genuine governance is exhibited with **no** separation (an agent that
governs itself with a bound it can override at will — that is self-restraint, not governance),
or with **no** mediation (a policy that never checks action — advice, not governance). The
meta-agency claim fails if governance is shown to be first-order agency of the same kind as
the governed (acting on the world, not on the agent). The excess-footprint origin fails if a
system with *no* surplus (effect-set = intention-set) still requires governance.
