## The theorems

### GT1 · The Governance Theorem — *No complete mediation → No governance* (Q10)
**Statement.** A policy that is not checked against *every* action before effect does not
govern — it advises. Complete mediation (GA3) is constitutive; a single unmediated path voids
the bound.

**Support: SUPPORTED for the constitutive core; the strong "every conceivable path" form runs
into GA5.** Two readings:
- *Constitutive reading:* remove complete mediation → some action escapes the policy → the
  policy is advisory, not governing. Follows from the reference-monitor condition (Anderson).
- *Deflationary perimeter:* a *partially* mediating governor still governs *what it mediates*
  — governance is degreed (Vol VII·02), and "complete" is the ideal a real governor approaches
  under its variety (GA5).

**Verdict on "จริงไหม": Yes** — non-trivially: it locates governance's constitutive node
(mediation) and explains its signature failure (the bypass, GF1). Headline "No mediation → no
governance" is near-analytic; the substantive claim is **"a governor is only as complete as
its least-mediated boundary"** — falsifiable and, this session, *reproduced*. **Falsifier:**
governance that survives a permanent unmediated path.

### GT3 · The Requisite-Variety Theorem (Ashby, recovered as governance's ceiling)
**Statement.** A governor can *completely* govern only a governed whose variety it matches;
a governor poorer than the governed has a coverage gap, and the un-covered action is the
governance hole. **Support: SUPPORTED** (Ashby + the reproduced P0 as an un-enumerated path).
**Consequence:** complete governance is achieved not by a smarter rule but by **shrinking the
governed's variety to the governor's** (least privilege, deny-by-default, minimal TCB) — i.e.
**construct, don't audit**, the governance-layer image of the Security Theory's constructive
result and of HRU-undecidability. **Falsifier:** a governor completely governing a strictly
richer governed.

### GT4 · The Security-Specialization Theorem
**Statement.** The Theory of Capability Escalation (recovered this session) **is** Vol VII
restricted to the domain of *authority*: authority-conservation = complete mediation (GA3);
the impossibility triangle = the failure of GA3; the reference monitor = the Governor; minimal
TCB = requisite variety by construction (GT3). **Support: SUPPORTED** (every security concept
maps onto a governance node, Vol VII·03). **Consequence:** the P0 fixes were governance theory
enacted before the theory was written — Vol VII names what they instantiate, and generalizes
them: *every* actuator (not only authority-bearing ones) owes complete mediation by a governor
of sufficient variety. **Falsifier:** a security escalation that is not a governance-node
failure.

### GT5 · The Excess-Footprint Theorem (why governance is non-optional)
**Statement.** Because every execution's effects exceed its intention (Vol V XT5), there is
always a surplus no agent's commitment authored; that surplus can only be owned by a regulator
*beyond* the agent. Therefore **an acting system without governance has an unowned footprint**
— its side effects belong to no one and bind no one. **Support: SUPPORTED** (follows from Vol
V). **Consequence:** governance is not friction added to agency; it is the *only* owner of
agency's surplus. Removing it does not free the agent — it orphans the harm. **Falsifier:** an
agent whose effect-set equals its intention-set (no surplus to govern) — unavailable in a
connected world (Vol V).

## Q9 · Open problems
1. **Legitimacy — the source of the norm (the open foundation).** *Why* is a governor's bound
   binding? Consent, tradition, rational agreement (Rawls/Scanlon), power? None grounds itself
   without circularity or regress. The governance-layer image of warrant's ground, value's
   authority, the first question, and induction — the stack's **one hole, seen the eighth and
   final way.** OPEN.
2. **The governor of the governor (regress).** Who governs the Governor? A higher governor
   (infinite regress), or a self-governing top (GA2 says self-governance is incomplete). Every
   governance hierarchy terminates in an un-governed governor or a circle. OPEN.
3. **Scalable oversight (GA5).** Governing a governed richer than the governor — the AI-safety
   crux; Ashby says it is impossible *completely*, so the question is how much partial
   governance suffices, and how to shrink the governed to fit. OPEN.
4. **Corrigibility.** Can an agent be governed to *accept* override/shutdown without resisting
   (since resisting override is instrumentally convergent, Vol II)? OPEN.
5. **Multi-agent / commons governance.** The excess footprint is shared across agents (Ostrom);
   governing a common footprint with no single governor is the collective-action problem. OPEN.
6. **Legitimate proxies (GF8).** Any enforceable policy is a proxy for the real harm; a proxy
   is Goodhart-gameable. Whether a non-gameable enforceable governance exists is UNKNOWN.

## The stack-closing statement

With Governance, the **Theory of Agency is complete as a system** — seven volumes, from what
an agent *is* (I) through what it *values* (II), *decides* (III), *plans* (IV), *executes*
(V), *learns* (VI), and is *governed by* (VII). Joined to the nine-theory Theory of Knowing at
the shared Observation node, the whole is a **figure-eight with every crossing theorized** —
Observation (input), Decision (forward output), Guidance (in-act), Learning (return),
Governance (the reflexive wrap) — and every node carries its constitutive **No-X→No-Y**.

And the whole rests on **one hole seen eight ways**: the ultimate ground (warrant), the first
question (inquiry), the origin of ends (goal), the authority of terminal value, the bindingness
of commitment, the problem of induction (learning), and the **legitimacy of the governor**
(governance). A cognitive-and-agentive system, complete in its middle, cannot manufacture its
own foundation — it cannot certify its own warrant, author its own values, ground its own
induction, or legitimate its own governor. **That eightfold openness, not any single theorem,
is the stack's final and most-warranted result:** the system knows exactly where it must
receive its foundation from outside itself — and building it (SkynetClaw) means choosing,
deliberately and accountably, what to put in each of those eight holes.

The pattern holds a final, ninth time — **determinate in the middle, open at the foundation**
— and Vol VII's distinctive contribution is to show that *this system's* live governance
failure is no longer GF1 (bypass, fixed) but **GF5 (requisite variety)**: a governor that must
grow its variety to match a governed that keeps acquiring capability — which is exactly the
paradigm's law (no capability without its governing invariant) restated as a theorem.
