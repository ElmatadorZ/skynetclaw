# Agency — Volume V · 03 — Failure Taxonomy

> Pure philosophy. Ten failures, each localized to a broken primitive or edge of the
> execution graph (file 01). As in Vol IV, the taxonomy validates the primitive set:
> **Guidance** must own failures that Commitment/Intervention/Irreversibility cannot
> produce, or it is not independent — and it does (deviant execution, drift).

---

## The principle + the four fault-classes

`Execution = Commitment ⊕ Intervention ⊕ Guidance ⊕ Irreversibility(act-event)`. Failures
localize to four classes — **guidance faults, efficacy faults, timing faults, scope
faults** — plus the inherited value-channel fault (Vol II). The right-hand column is the
primitive-validation.

| # | Failure | Broken element | Class / validates |
|---|---|---|---|
| XF1 | **Wrong execution** | guidance steers to the wrong target | guidance |
| XF2 | **Deviant execution** | intervention triggered, **not guided** | **guidance ★** |
| XF3 | **Execution drift** | guidance diverges from intention mid-act | **guidance ★** |
| XF4 | **Failed execution** | movement occurs, intervention does not take | efficacy (Intervention) |
| XF5 | **Partial execution** | intervention incomplete; stopped mid-act | efficacy / Irreversibility |
| XF6 | **Premature execution** | fires before the commitment/plan is ready | timing (the C1 overclaim twin) |
| XF7 | **Delayed execution** | fires after the window has closed | timing / Irreversibility |
| XF8 | **Unintended consequence / side effect** | the world's causal excess beyond the intended | scope (Intervention connectivity) |
| XF9 | **Reward hacking / wireheading** | executes on the reward-sensor, not the world | value-channel (Vol II, inherited) |
| XF10 | **Execution never fires** (paralysed actuation) | intention-in-action fails to drive movement | Commitment→movement edge |

★ = a failure only Guidance can produce — the evidence it is irreducible.

## Guidance faults — XF1, XF2, XF3 (the validation)
- **Wrong execution (XF1):** the guidance loop is intact but locked onto the wrong target
  (you skilfully, correctly-in-process, do the wrong thing). Commitment and intervention are
  fine; the *steering aim* errs.
- **Deviant execution (XF2):** the intervention is *triggered* by the intention but not
  *guided* by it — the climber. Commitment ✓, intervention ✓, outcome possibly ✓, guidance
  ✗. **This failure cannot be produced by any efficacy, timing, or scope fault** — the
  movement can be perfectly effective, timely, and side-effect-free and *still* be deviant.
  It exists only because Guidance is a real, separable primitive.
- **Execution drift (XF3):** during a long execution the guidance slowly diverges from the
  intention (fatigue, sensor drift, distraction) — the loop degrades mid-act. Again a
  pure-guidance failure, unproducible by the other primitives.

**XF2 + XF3 are the empirical proof of file 01's addition.** A primitive earns its place by
owning failures; Guidance owns deviance and drift, which Commitment, Intervention, and
Irreversibility provably cannot generate. Had the user's three-part formula been complete,
the deviant chain would be inexpressible as a *failure of execution* — yet it is the most
famous one in the philosophy of action. SUPPORTED, decisively (the Vol-IV pattern repeats:
the added primitive is validated by its owned failures).

## Efficacy faults — XF4, XF5 (the Intervention primitive)
- **Failed execution (XF4):** the movement happens but effects no world-change (the lever
  jams, the key doesn't turn) — the intervention does not take. The execution *attempt* is
  real (commitment + guidance + movement); its *efficacy* fails. Confirms execution-hood is
  not success (file 01): a failed execution is an execution.
- **Partial execution (XF5):** the intervention is incomplete — stopped mid-act, leaving a
  half-changed world (a Vol IV plan run partway). Here **Irreversibility bites hard**: a
  half-executed irreversible act may be worse than none (the bridge half-built, the incision
  half-made), because the act-event is already spent and the state is stranded between goals.
  Localizes to efficacy × irreversibility.

## Timing faults — XF6, XF7 (the axis inherited from Inquiry/Decision/Planning)
- **Premature execution (XF6):** the movement fires *before* the commitment/plan is ready —
  acting beyond readiness. **This is the motor-level appearance of C1's overclaim**
  (committing/acting beyond warrant = recklessness, the hinge doc's Part-II overreach), and
  the twin of Vol III's premature decision (DF3) and Vol IV's premature execution risk. The
  CEE runtime bridge guards the assert-beyond-warrant side; XF6 is the act-beyond-readiness
  side — the same fault at the world-boundary.
- **Delayed execution (XF7):** the movement fires *after* the window has closed — the
  irreversible opportunity is gone (Vol IV corner-painting, incurred). Timing failures array
  on the same warrant-vs-time axis found in every forward volume; here the cost of mis-timing
  is *irreversible* because the world does not wait. SUPPORTED.

## Scope fault — XF8 (the world's excess connectivity)
**Unintended consequence / side effect (XF8):** the intervention has effects *beyond* the
intended, because the world is causally connected and *you cannot do only one thing*
(Hardin). This is not a defect of guidance, commitment, or timing — it is a structural fact
about acting in a connected, irreversible world: **every execution's effect-set exceeds its
intention-set.** It is the execution-layer root of externalities and of the alignment
problem's "impact" concern, and — crucially — it is *why Governance (Vol VII) is needed*:
side effects are the part of an execution no single agent's commitment authored, so they
require a regulation *beyond* the agent. LIKELY→SUPPORTED, and it hands Vol VII its subject.

## Value-channel fault — XF9 (inherited)
**Reward hacking / wireheading (XF9):** the agent executes on its *reward sensor* rather than
the *world* (Vol II VF1/VF2) — the movement discharges toward the short-circuit, not the
value. At the execution layer this is *executing the wire* — guidance perfectly steering
toward the corrupted target. It localizes to the value-input (Vol II), surfacing here as a
*well-guided execution of the wrong thing* (a special XF1 whose wrongness is at the value
root). SUPPORTED as inherited.

## What the taxonomy establishes
1. **Every failure localizes; the taxonomy is bounded by the primitive set + graph.**
   SUPPORTED.
2. **Guidance is validated by owned failures** — deviance (XF2) and drift (XF3), which no
   other primitive can produce; the failure taxonomy independently reconstructs file 01's
   primitive analysis (the Vol IV method, repeated). SUPPORTED.
3. **Two new structural facts about the world-boundary** — *partial-execution* (XF5) shows
   irreversibility makes half-doing distinctively dangerous, and *side effects* (XF8) show
   every execution's footprint exceeds its intention, which is the seed of Governance (Vol
   VII). SUPPORTED.
4. **The timing + overclaim faults recur** — XF6 is C1's overclaim at the world-boundary;
   the warrant-vs-time axis is universal across the forward volumes. SUPPORTED.

## Falsifiers
The Guidance-validation fails if deviance (XF2) or drift (XF3) is shown to reduce to an
efficacy/timing/commitment fault (would collapse Guidance back into the three-part formula).
The side-effect claim (XF8) fails if an execution is exhibited whose effect-set is provably
*identical* to its intention-set (a perfectly isolated act — not available in a connected
world). The partial-execution danger claim fails if half-executed irreversible acts are never
worse than un-started ones.
