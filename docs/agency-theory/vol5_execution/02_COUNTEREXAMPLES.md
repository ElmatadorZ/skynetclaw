# Agency — Volume V · 02 — Counterexamples & Boundary Cases

> Pure philosophy. Ten cases, each ruled **Execution** or **Not-Execution** with the
> discriminating reason, tested against `Execution = Commitment ⊕ Intervention ⊕ Guidance
> ⊕ Irreversibility`. The cases are also the evidence that Guidance is the load-bearing
> new primitive (file 01).

---

## The ten verdicts

| Case | Verdict | Discriminating reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Reflex** | **Not-Execution** | no commitment, no intentional guidance — a triggered arc |
| **Spasm** | **Not-Execution** | pure movement; no commitment, no guidance (the deviant chain's mechanism) |
| **Accident** | **Not-Execution** | world-change with no commitment/guidance |
| **Lucky success** | **Not-Execution** | outcome reached without a guided doing (outcome ≠ execution) |
| **Deviant causal chain** | **Not-Execution** | commitment + intervention + outcome, **guidance absent** — the canonical case |
| **Automation (script)** | **Derived-Execution** | guided, but the commitment is the designer's — borrowed agency |
| **Habit** | **Execution** (compiled) | standing commitment + cached guidance — fluent, not deliberative |
| **PID controller** | **Not-Execution** (bare) / **Derived** (in an agent) | guidance without commitment — control, not execution |
| **Robot arm** | **Derived-Execution** | genuine guidance realizing a borrowed commitment |
| **Autopilot** | **Derived-Execution** | guided intervention discharging the operator's commitment |

## Dissected — the cases that carry weight

### Deviant causal chain — *Guidance is necessary* (the crux case)
The climber (file 00/01): committed to loosen, the wish causes the loosening, the man
falls. All of Commitment + Intervention + Outcome are present; only **Guidance** is absent —
and the verdict is decisively **Not-Execution** (he did not *do* the dropping; it happened
*to* him via his own nerves). This single case does what the shopping-list did for Vol IV:
it proves the mission's proposed formula (`Committed Intervention that changes the world`)
**insufficient**, and names the missing primitive. It is the counterexample the *user
supplied*, turned into the volume's foundation. SUPPORTED, decisively.

### Spasm & Reflex & Accident — *the unguided floor*
A spasm is movement with neither commitment nor guidance; a reflex is a triggered arc
(guidance of a sort, but no *commitment* — the knee does not intend); an accident is a
world-change with no agentive origin. All three are **Not-Execution**, and they populate the
floor beneath execution — the *unguided or uncommitted* movements. They mirror Vol I's
sub-agency floor and Vol IV's reactive floor: below commitment-guided doing, there is
motion but no execution. Note the reflex is instructive: it has a *control loop* (the arc)
yet is not execution, because the loop realizes no *committed* intention — confirming that
Guidance alone is not sufficient either; **Guidance + Commitment together** are the
differentia. SUPPORTED.

### Lucky success — *outcome ≠ execution* (Vol III's resulting, at the motor level)
A dart thrown wildly that hits the bullseye reaches the outcome with no guided execution —
the hand did not *steer* the dart to center. **Not-Execution** (of a bullseye; it was an
execution of *a throw*, which happened to land well). This re-imports Vol III Q6's
`outcome:decision :: truth:belief` one layer down: `outcome : execution :: truth : belief`
— the outcome is what execution *aims at*, never what makes movement *be* execution. A lucky
success and a deviant chain are twins: both reach the outcome without the guided doing; the
difference is only that the deviant chain was *committed* and the lucky success may not be.
SUPPORTED.

### PID controller & Robot arm & Autopilot — *Execution ≠ Control; and derived execution*
A bare PID loop **guides** beautifully (feedback correction toward a setpoint) but commits
to nothing it authored — so it is **control, not execution** (the setpoint is given). Placed
*inside an agent* (robot arm, autopilot) discharging an operator's committed plan, it becomes
**derived execution** — genuine guidance realizing a *borrowed* commitment. This is the exact
mirror of Vol I's derived agency and Vol II's borrowed ends: **machine execution is real
guidance with an empty commitment-root, filled from outside.** It also sharpens the boundary
the user drew (Execution ≠ Control): control is the *guidance mechanism*; execution is
*control harnessed to discharge a commitment*. A thermostat has the mechanism and not the
commitment; a robot arm under a plan has both (the second borrowed). SUPPORTED — and the
honest note for any artificial system: its executions are guided by it but *committed by its
operator* (the origin-of-ends problem, Vol II, appears here as the origin-of-commitment).

### Automation & Habit — *derived and compiled execution*
Automation (a running script) is **derived execution** — guided by cached rules, committed by
the author. Habit is **compiled execution** — a standing commitment whose guidance has been
cached into fluent, non-deliberative control (Vol IV's compiled plan, now discharged). Both
are genuine executions in their mode; neither requires moment-to-moment deliberation. They
fill the *fluent* stratum (Vol IV red team) at the execution layer: deliberative execution
(careful action) / compiled execution (habit, skill) / derived execution (machine) /
non-execution (reflex, spasm). The strata match the planning strata one-to-one. SUPPORTED.

## What the counterexamples establish
1. **Guidance is necessary and load-bearing** — the deviant chain (guidance absent) and the
   lucky success (guidance absent) are the two clean Not-Executions that share everything
   *but* guidance with a real execution. SUPPORTED.
2. **Guidance alone is not sufficient — Commitment is co-necessary** — the reflex and the
   bare PID *guide* without executing, for lack of a committed intention. The differentia is
   the *pair*. SUPPORTED.
3. **Derived/compiled execution is real** — machines and habits execute (guidance present)
   on borrowed/standing commitments; the commitment-root can be empty-and-filled or
   cached, exactly as in Vols I–IV. SUPPORTED.
4. **outcome ≠ execution** — lucky success reaches the target with no guided doing;
   `outcome : execution :: truth : belief`. SUPPORTED.

## Falsifiers
Any verdict is refuted by showing the case has (or lacks) the discriminator claimed — e.g.
exhibit a deviant chain that *is* a genuine execution (would refute Guidance-necessity), or
a bare thermostat correctly said to *execute a commitment* (would collapse Execution into
Control). The derived-execution reading fails if machine action is shown to require an
*intrinsic* commitment (then robots never execute, and "execution" excludes all artifacts —
a bullet the volume declines to bite, locating the borrowed-commitment question as open).
