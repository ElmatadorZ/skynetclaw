## The candidate theorems, adjudicated

The user proposed testing `No Irreversible Effect → No Execution` and `Execution =
Committed Intervention that changes the world`. Both are refined by the recovery.

### XT1 · `Execution = Committed Intervention that changes the world` — **FALSIFIED as sufficient**
Refuted by the deviant chain (files 00–02): the climber satisfies *committed intervention
that changes the world* and does **not** execute, because **Guidance** is absent. The
formula is correct in three parts (Commitment, Intervention, world-change) and missing the
one the deviant chain isolates. Corrected:

> **XT1′ · Execution = Commitment ⊕ Intervention ⊕ Guidance ⊕ Irreversibility(act-event).**

Support: SUPPORTED as *necessary* conditions (each owns a failure, file 03); the *complete
anti-deviance* form of Guidance is OPEN (below). Verdict on the user's formula: **FALSIFIED
as stated, repaired by adding Guidance** — the exact Vol IV pattern (a nearly-right formula
missing one failure-owning primitive). **Falsifier:** show the deviant chain *is* an
execution (guidance not needed) or that guidance reduces to the other three.

### XT2 · `No Irreversible Effect → No Execution` — **TRUE, refined (act-event, not state)**
The necessary irreversibility of an execution is the **irreversible act-event** — the doing
*happened*, the now is spent, the opportunity is gone — *not* necessarily a permanent
world-state change (reversible-state executions exist: I flip a switch I could flip back, but
the flipping irreversibly occurred). So:

> **XT2′ · Every execution incurs an irreversible act-event (agency's now is spent);
> No irreversible occurrence → No execution.** The state-effect may be reversible; the
> *act's occurrence* never is.

Support: SUPPORTED. This grounds Vol IV's irreversibility at its *source*: irreversibility is
not a property the *world* contingently has, it is the price the *act* necessarily pays —
time-as-consumed (Vol IV's master resource) discharged at the world-boundary. Verdict on the
user's theorem: **TRUE**, with "effect" read as the act-event's occurrence. **Falsifier:** an
execution that leaves *no* trace in the fixed past — not the now spent, not a resource used,
not a state touched (a genuinely costless, un-happened doing — a contradiction).

### XT3 · The Payment Theorem — *execution is where the internal is exchanged for the external, irreversibly*
**Statement.** Everything upstream of execution (Value, Commitment, Plan) is **internal and
revisable**; at execution the commitment is **discharged against the world** and becomes an
irreversible act-event. Execution is the unique **payment point** where the reversible
internal is exchanged for the irreversible external. **Support: SUPPORTED.** **Consequence:**
this is *why* Vol IV recovered irreversibility — it originates here; and it explains the
whole stack's asymmetry (Part I never paid, because knowing never crosses this boundary).
**Falsifier:** a stack stage before execution that is itself irreversible (would move the
payment point upstream) — internal states are revisable, so none is known.

### XT4 · The Fusion Theorem — *execution couples both directions of fit in real time*
**Statement.** Guidance *is* Observation running inside the act (the world→mind sensor
correcting the mind→world movement continuously). So execution is where the two directions of
fit **fuse at the finest grain** — tighter than Decision (which combined belief and value
*between* acts, Vol III). **Support: LIKELY.** **Consequence:** the deviant-chain problem
lives *only* here because it is precisely the question *whether the sense→act loop was closed
during the act*; and it locates the third and finest bridge between the loops (Observation =
input bridge; Decision = output-combination bridge; **Guidance = in-act fusion bridge**).
**Falsifier:** a paradigm execution with provably no in-act feedback (fully ballistic) that
is nonetheless non-deviant and fully guided — the open-loop limit tests this and is treated as
degenerate.

### XT5 · The Excess-Footprint Theorem — *every execution's effects exceed its intention*
**Statement.** In a causally connected world, an execution's effect-set strictly exceeds its
intention-set (side effects, XF8) — *you cannot do only one thing*. **Support: SUPPORTED.**
**Consequence:** the surplus is the part of an execution no agent's commitment authored, so it
cannot be governed by the agent's own intention — it requires a regulation *beyond* the agent.
**This hands Vol VII (Governance) its subject**: governance is the management of the
irreversible excess footprint of execution (externalities, impact, the parts of acting no one
chose). **Falsifier:** an execution with effect-set equal to intention-set (a perfectly
isolated act — unavailable in a connected world).

## Open problems
1. **The deviant-chain problem (the volume's central OPEN).** Guidance is the *necessary*
   ingredient, but no condition on guidance is agreed to exclude *every* deviant chain —
   deviance can recur *inside* the guidance loop (a "correcting" sensor that corrects via a
   second deviant path). Davidson posed it; it remains unsolved. The volume names the
   primitive; it does not close the problem. OPEN.
2. **The origin of commitment for derived executors.** A robot/autopilot executes (guidance
   present) on a *borrowed* commitment; whether that is *genuine* execution or the operator's
   execution-at-a-distance is the execution-layer form of Vol II's borrowed-ends /
   origin-of-ends. OPEN.
3. **Basic vs derived actions (the regress).** Some executions are done "directly" (raising
   an arm) and others "by" doing something else (flipping a switch by moving a finger);
   where the "basic action" bottoms out — and whether it too has guidance — is a classic open
   question (Danto). OPEN.
4. **Mental action.** Are purely internal acts (deciding, imagining, attending) executions?
   They guide and commit but touch no external world and are more reversible — the degenerate
   internal limit of execution. Its status is UNKNOWN.
5. **Trying vs doing.** Is there a "trying" (a pure executive act) that is present even in
   failed/paralysed execution (XF4, XF10)? If so, trying may be the true minimal executive
   primitive, with movement contingent. Contested (O'Shaughnessy, Hornsby). OPEN.
6. **Collective execution.** When many agents' movements jointly effect one intervention (a
   crew, a market), whose execution is it, and where is the guidance? Deferred to Vol VII.
   OPEN (scoped out).

## Settled vs open (honest ledger)
- **Settled (SUPPORTED):** XT1′ (the four-part primitive, Guidance added); XT2′
  (act-event irreversibility); XT3 (execution = the payment point); XT5 (excess footprint →
  Governance's subject); the failure taxonomy's Guidance-validation.
- **Settled (LIKELY):** XT4 (guidance = in-act fusion of both directions of fit).
- **FALSIFIED (a genuine result):** the user's `Committed Intervention that changes the
  world` as *sufficient* (missing Guidance); the strong reading of `No Irreversible Effect`
  as *permanent state change* (only the act-event is necessarily irreversible).
- **Open / UNKNOWN:** the deviant-chain problem (1, the central one); origin of borrowed
  commitment (2); basic-action regress (3); mental action (4); trying-vs-doing (5); collective
  execution (6).

The pattern holds a seventh time — **determinate in the middle, open at the frontier** — and
Vol V's frontier is unusually deep: the **deviant-chain problem is a *named, classic,
unsolved* problem**, so the volume's honest posture is to *recover the missing primitive
(Guidance)* while *explicitly not claiming to have solved* what the discipline has not. That
restraint is itself the C1 discipline the whole stack enforces: name what you have, mark what
you do not, and do not present a located primitive as a closed problem.
