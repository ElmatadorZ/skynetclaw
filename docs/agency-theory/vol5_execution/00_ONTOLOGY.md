# Agency — Volume V · 00 — The Ontology of Execution

> Pure philosophy. Primary question: **"What is an execution?"** — sharpened to **"what
> makes a movement become an action, and an action become an event in the world?"** Not
> "how do we execute." No control algorithm (PID, MPC) as an axiom. Part II, Volume V —
> the **payment point**: where the internal (Value → Commitment → Plan, all reversible)
> is exchanged for the external world at a non-refundable price, and where Vol IV's
> recovered *irreversibility* is actually incurred. Objectives: ontology + boundary.
> Tags: SUPPORTED / LIKELY / SPECULATIVE / UNKNOWN / FALSIFIED. Sourcing caveat:
> attributions recalled from philosophy of action, control theory, cybernetics; names
> index positions.

**The problem that organizes the volume (Davidson).** The causal theory of action says:
an action is a movement *caused by* the agent's intention. But causation is not enough —
the **deviant causal chain** refutes it. Davidson's climber: he wants to drop the man he
is holding; the *wish* so unnerves him that his hand *loosens* and the man falls. The
intention *caused* the movement and the intended outcome *occurred* — yet he did not
*execute* the dropping; it was a nervous spasm that happened to succeed. So:

> **Committed intention + causation + intended outcome is NOT sufficient for execution.**
> Something more is required — and identifying it is the whole task of Vol V.

---

## Primary question · What is an execution?

Traditions, from their sides:
- **Control theory / cybernetics.** Execution is the closed-loop regulation of a process
  toward a setpoint — *guided* actuation with feedback. — Names the *mechanism* (guidance)
  but not the *agentive* status (a thermostat regulates without executing anything it
  committed to).
- **Philosophy of action (Anscombe, Davidson, Frankfurt, Bishop).** An execution is a
  movement that is the agent's *doing* — caused by the intention **in the right way**, i.e.
  *guided* by it, not merely triggered. The unsolved residue is specifying "the right way"
  (the deviant-chain problem).
- **Pearl (intervention).** `do(X=x)` — a causal setting of the world. — Names the *causal
  content* of what execution effects, but is agent-neutral (a tsunami intervenes; it does
  not execute).

**Recovered core (SUPPORTED, with an OPEN residue):** *an execution is a committed
intervention on the world realized through the intention's ongoing **guidance** of the
movement, incurring an irreversible act-event.* The differentia against mere
caused-movement is **guidance/control**: in execution the intention does not just *start*
the movement, it *steers* it — the unfolding is counterfactually sensitive to the goal, so
deviations would be corrected. The deviant chain fails exactly here: the loosening was
*triggered* by the intention but not *guided* by it (no monitoring-and-correcting toward
"loosen the grip"). What remains OPEN is the *complete* specification of "guided in the
right way" that rules out *every* deviant chain — Davidson's problem, still unsolved
(file 04).

**Direction of fit + the asymmetry (the volume's pivot).** Execution is the **mind→world
output boundary** — the point where the internal crosses into the external. Its
world→mind twin at the input boundary is **Observation** (the world writing into the mind).
The two boundaries are **not symmetric**: perception *adds* to the mind (reversible,
non-consuming); execution *spends* into the world (irreversible, consuming). This is Vol
IV's kernel, now located at its source — irreversibility is not a property plans *have*, it
is a price executions *pay*.

## The ten entities, recovered

| Entity | Minimal definition | Relation to execution |
|---|---|---|
| **Movement** | physical/bodily motion (kinematics) | the *material* of execution; movement alone (spasm) is not execution |
| **Behaviour** | patterned movement, possibly as-if goal-directed | broader than action; teleonomic behaviour (Vol I) is not execution |
| **Action** | a movement that is the agent's doing, answerable to "why?" (Anscombe) | execution is action *in progress under guidance*; action is its category |
| **Execution** | a committed intervention realized through guided movement, incurring an irreversible act-event | the subject of the volume |
| **Event** | a change that *happens* in the world | actions are a subspecies of events (Davidson); execution makes an internal commitment *become* an event |
| **Outcome** | the resulting world-state | execution ≠ outcome (deviant chain: right outcome, no execution) |
| **Effect** | the causal consequence(s) of the movement | includes intended + side effects (the world's excess connectivity) |
| **Intervention** | `do(X=x)` — a causal setting of the world (Pearl) | the *causal content* execution effects; agent-neutral (nature intervenes) |
| **Control** | ongoing regulation of a process toward a setpoint (feedback) | the *guidance mechanism* execution uses; control without commitment (thermostat) is not execution |
| **Agency** | the capacity to act for ends (Vol I) | execution is agency *discharged* — the moment capacity becomes actuality |

## Boundary matrix — Execution ≠ …

| Execution ≠ | Shares | **Lacks / differs** (the discriminator) |
|---|---|---|
| **Movement** | bodily motion | *commitment + guidance* — a spasm moves without either (the deviant chain is spasm wearing an outcome) |
| **Outcome** | aims at a world-state | *the guided doing* — a lucky success reaches the outcome with no execution; execution can miss the outcome |
| **Intervention** | changes the world causally | *agentive guidance* — a landslide effects `do(·)` but executes nothing; execution is a *guided* intervention |
| **Control** | feedback guidance | *commitment* — a thermostat controls with no committed intention it is realizing (Vol I minimal agent) |
| **Behaviour** | observable activity | *committed, guided doing* — teleonomic/reflexive behaviour is unguided-by-commitment |

**The two discriminators that carry the matrix (SUPPORTED):**
1. **Guidance** separates Execution from *Movement, Intervention, Behaviour, Outcome* —
   these can occur *caused-but-unguided* (spasm, landslide, reflex, luck); execution is the
   *steered* realization. This is the deviant-chain lesson generalized.
2. **Commitment** separates Execution from *Control* — a servo guides without committing;
   execution discharges a commitment (Vol III) it inherited through a plan (Vol IV).

Note what is *not* a clean discriminator: **Intervention** and **Outcome** — because
execution shares the causal-change with a mere intervention and the target-state with a
mere outcome; only *guided-committed-doing* separates it. This is why the user's candidate
`Execution = Committed Intervention that changes the world` is on the right track but
insufficient — it names Commitment + Intervention + world-change but omits **Guidance**,
the very thing the deviant chain shows is missing (file 01 proves this).

## Falsifiers
The core fails if a genuine execution is exhibited with no guidance at all (a pure
triggered movement we would nonetheless call a proper execution — the deviant chain is the
standing test and is judged *not* execution). The commitment-discriminator fails if a
servo/thermostat is correctly said to *execute* (with no committed intention). The
mind→world-boundary claim fails if a genuine execution effects no external change of any
kind (pure internal act — treated as the degenerate limit in file 01).
