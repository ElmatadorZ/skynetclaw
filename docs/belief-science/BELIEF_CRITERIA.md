# Belief Criteria — necessary and sufficient conditions

> Derived by intersecting the surviving operational models (BELIEF_TAXONOMY). A property is
> *necessary* only if it appears in **every** surviving model; *sufficient* only if its
> presence alone forces belief-hood. Tags: SUPPORTED / LIKELY / SPECULATIVE / UNKNOWN /
> FALSIFIED. Persistence is tested explicitly (OBJECTIVE 4).

## Candidate properties (from OBJECTIVE 5) and their status
| Property | In every surviving model? | Necessary? | Sufficient? | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Content / aboutness** (is *about* a state of affairs; has satisfaction/truth conditions) | Yes (T1–T11 all ascribe content, even implicitly) | **Necessary** | No | SUPPORTED |
| **Dispositional behavioural influence** (disposes to act/bet/predict as-if-content) | Yes — the sole universal (T2,T4,T5,T8,T9 make it definitional; T1,T6 include causal role) | **Necessary** | No | SUPPORTED |
| **Counterfactual / dispositional stability** (reproducible across a range of triggers, not a one-off) | Yes — distinguishes belief from a fleeting guess (implicit in "disposition", "sufficient statistic", "standing") | **Necessary (minimal)** | No | LIKELY |
| **Revisability in principle** (is the *kind* of state evidence could change) | Central in T5/T6/T8/T9; implicit elsewhere | **Necessary for *empirical* belief** | No | LIKELY |
| **Predictive power** (improves prediction of experience) | T5/T6/T8/T9 make it central; NOT required for evaluative/past-directed belief | No (not universal) | No | LIKELY (empirical only) |
| **Persistence as a stored object** | **No** — denied by T2,T4,T5,T7,T9,T11-occurrent | **NOT necessary** | **NOT sufficient** | SUPPORTED |
| **Update rule (active mechanism)** | T6/T8/T9 require it; T2/T4/T7 do not (revisable ≠ actively-updating) | No | No | LIKELY |
| **Compression / generalization** | property of *efficient* beliefs, not belief-hood (a belief about one particular fact needn't generalize) | No | No | SUPPORTED |
| **Coherence** (Dutch-book / logical consistency) | a norm of *rational* belief (T5/T6), violated by real believers | No (normative, not constitutive) | No | SUPPORTED |
| **Action selection** (must drive a choice) | subsumed by dispositional influence; not a separate necessity | No | No | LIKELY |

## Necessary conditions (the intersection) · SUPPORTED unless noted
A state is a belief **only if**:
- **N1 · Content** — it is *about* something (has satisfaction conditions). SUPPORTED.
- **N2 · Dispositional influence** — it disposes the organism to act/predict/bet *as-if* that
  content, in belief-relevant conditions. SUPPORTED (the one universal).
- **N3 · Counterfactual stability** — that disposition is *reproducible* across a range of
  conditions/probes (not a single accident). LIKELY.
- **N4 · Revisability-in-principle** — some possible evidence *could* alter it (for empirical
  belief). LIKELY.

## Sufficient conditions · LIKELY
No single property is sufficient. The **conjunction N1 ∧ N2 ∧ N3** is *jointly sufficient*
for ascribing (at least dispositional) belief: a state that is *about* X and *reproducibly
disposes* the organism to act/predict as-if-X **is** a belief on every surviving model
(representationalist, dispositional, interpretationist, Bayesian-operational, POMDP). Adding
N4 upgrades it to *empirical/revisable* belief. **SPECULATIVE** at the edges (finkish
dispositions, holism — see REDTEAM).

## The persistence verdict (OBJECTIVE 4) · SUPPORTED
- **Persistence-as-stored-object is NEITHER necessary NOR sufficient.**
  - *Not sufficient:* a persisted record that influences nothing (a forgotten note in a
    drawer) satisfies persistence but not N2 → not a belief. (Every functional/dispositional
    account agrees.)
  - *Not necessary:* occurrent beliefs (T11), tacit beliefs (T7), POMDP belief-states
    recomputed from history (T9), and dispositional/credence beliefs (T2/T5) all satisfy
    N1–N3 with **no stored belief-token**.
- **What IS a weak persistence requirement:** N3 (counterfactual stability) requires the
  *disposition* to persist — but that persistence can be realized by a **stored token OR by
  reproducible re-derivation from a persistent model/history** (as in T9). So the substrate
  that persists may be the *generator*, not the *belief*. → "persistence" reappears one level
  down, in the *carrier of the disposition*, not in the belief object.

## One-line criterion (LIKELY synthesis)
> **A belief is a contentful, counterfactually-stable disposition to act and predict as-if a
> state of affairs holds — however that disposition is carried (stored or re-derived), and
> whether or not it is ever revised.**
