# Belief Theorems — deriving every surviving claim from Axiom A

> Each prior surviving finding re-derived from the single axiom (BELIEF_AXIOMS §Axiom A). If a
> derivation fails, the axiom is incomplete (checked in REDTEAM_OF_UNIFICATION). No inference
> beyond what A licenses. Tags: SUPPORTED / LIKELY / SPECULATIVE / UNKNOWN.

> **A** = *Belief is a counterfactually-invariant relation between a system's world-tracking
> states and its actions, existing exactly where and at the grain at which that invariance is
> exploitable for prediction and intervention.*

## T1 · Belief is a disposition, not an object (s1) · SUPPORTED
A relation defined by counterfactuals ("were condition C, action A") **is** a disposition; a
disposition is not a stored token. ∴ belief is dispositional. ∎

## T2 · The disposition is counterfactually stable, storage-independent (s2, s11) · SUPPORTED
"Invariant" **means** the relation reproduces across conditions = stability. A relation is fixed
by its counterfactual profile, not by *how* the profile is realized ⇒ any substrate that
reproduces the profile instantiates the same belief. ∴ stability is essential; **persistence of a
token is neither necessary** (re-derivation reproduces the invariance) **nor sufficient** (a
stored token with no invariant relation to action is not a belief). ∎

## T3 · Belief has content (s3) · SUPPORTED
The relation is *keyed to* the world-conditions it covaries with; that keying **is** its content
(what it is about, its satisfaction conditions). No separate posit needed. ∎

## T4 · Direction of fit is mind-to-world (s4) · LIKELY
A is asymmetric: on the **input** arm the *world* sets the state (the state *tracks/covaries*); on
the **output** arm the *state* sets the *action*. "State covaries with world" = the state changes
to match the world = mind-to-world. (Contrast: a state that sets a *world-target* on its output =
desire.) ∴ direction of fit falls out of the relation's structure. *LIKELY, not SUPPORTED,
because A's world-tracking asymmetry is strong-but-not-exceptionless (besires — REDTEAM).* ∎

## T5 · Revisability of empirical belief (s5) · LIKELY (restricted theorem)
Among the interventions the invariance is defined over, some act on the **input** (evidence). For
*empirical* belief, do(evidence) shifts the state (the invariance re-keys). ∴ empirical belief is
revisable. A belief whose invariance is *insensitive* to any evidence-intervention is the limiting
(non-empirical / sealed) case — still belief by A, but not empirical. ∴ revisability is a theorem
**restricted** to empirical belief, not an axiom. ∎

## T6 · Observable only through counterfactual behaviour (s6) · SUPPORTED
An *invariance* is by definition a fact about *varied* conditions; a single actual behaviour
cannot exhibit it. ∴ to detect the belief-relation one must **manipulate conditions** and observe
the invariant response = counterfactual/behavioural observation. Internals are not required (A is
relational). ∎

## T7 · Existence inferable, content underdetermined / coarse individuation (s7) · SUPPORTED
Finitely many manipulations reveal *that* an invariance holds (existence) but fix its input-keying
only up to the manipulations run ⇒ content is pinned only to a **holistic equivalence class**;
individuation is as coarse as the grain of A. ∴ existence SUPPORTED, exact content
underdetermined. ∎

## T8 · Belief is a cause = invariance under intervention (s8) · SUPPORTED (state) / UNKNOWN (content)
A *is* an invariance-under-intervention relation; under the interventionist criterion,
invariance-under-do() **is** causation. ∴ the belief-**state** is a difference-maker (cause);
epiphenomenalism-of-the-state is excluded. Whether the *content* vs the *vehicle* carries the
invariance is not decided by A ⇒ **UNKNOWN**. ∎

## T9 · The disposition/cause identity (dissolves the apparent tension) · SUPPORTED
T1 (disposition) and T8 (cause) are **the same fact** under A: a counterfactually-invariant
relation is *both* a disposition (counterfactuals about behaviour) *and* a cause (counterfactuals
about intervention). No two objects; one invariance. ∴ "belief is a disposition" and "belief
causes behaviour" do not conflict — they are one claim viewed on two counterfactual axes. ∎

## T10 · Warrant = difference-making; parsimony at the periphery (s9) · SUPPORTED
A posits belief *only where the invariance is exploitable for prediction/intervention*. Where no
manipulation reveals an exploitable invariance, A ascribes no belief. ∴ the concept is warranted
exactly by predictive/interventional difference — no difference, no belief (idle-wheel excision). ∎

## T11 · Cluster kind: hard core + soft periphery (s10) · LIKELY
"At the grain where the invariance holds" is graded: **strong, robust** invariance (direction-of-
fit + evidence-sensitive covariation) = the hard core; **weak/partial** invariance = the fuzzy
periphery (the liminal cases, where one arm of the relation degrades). ∴ A *generates* a cluster
with a robust centre and graded edges — an HPC kind, no classical essence. ∎

## T12 · The belief/knowledge boundary is external (not derivable) · SUPPORTED
A concerns the *internal* invariant relation. **Truth** and **anti-luck justification** are
world-side facts *outside* the relation. ∴ knowledge = belief + external conditions is **not**
derivable from A — correctly, since knowledge is not belief. A predicts exactly that the
belief/knowledge line is unobservable from behaviour (matching s7/Mission II). ∎
