# Liminal Cases — the edges where "belief" strains

> A catalogue of cases that sit *on the boundary* of belief (OBJECTIVE 4). **Recovered, not
> explained or resolved.** Each: what makes it liminal (which belief-criteria it satisfies vs
> fails). Criteria referenced: content, direction-of-fit, evidence-sensitivity, behavioural
> disposition, endorsement. Tags: SUPPORTED (attested phenomenon/debate) / LIKELY / UNKNOWN.

## L1 · Implicit / tacit belief · SUPPORTED
Beliefs never explicitly entertained yet dispositionally present (the "zebras don't wear
overcoats" case). Satisfies disposition + content; fails explicit endorsement/occurrence. Live
question: which of infinitely-many entailed propositions are "really" believed.

## L2 · Alief (Gendler) · SUPPORTED
Automatic, associative, belief-*discordant* states: standing on a glass skywalk one *believes*
it is safe yet *alieves* danger (sweats, hesitates). Satisfies behavioural disposition + affect;
**conflicts with the avowed belief** → shows belief-disposition and endorsed-belief can come
apart. Liminal between belief, expectation, and emotion.

## L3 · Self-deception · SUPPORTED
Holding (or forming) a belief against evidence one possesses, apparently motivated. Paradox:
how can one both grasp the evidence and believe its contrary? Positions: two systems / partitioned
belief / non-doxastic avowal. Strains the evidence-sensitivity criterion.

## L4 · Delusion · SUPPORTED (and its *belief-status* is itself disputed)
Clinically, "a fixed false belief resistant to counter-evidence." But whether delusions **are
beliefs** is contested (Bortolotti): they often fail evidence-sensitivity and action-guidance
(some patients don't act on the delusion — "double bookkeeping"). Doxastic vs metacognitive vs
perceptual-inference accounts. Liminal between belief, imagining, and perceptual anomaly.

## L5 · Confabulation · SUPPORTED
Fluent, confident assertion of a *reason that is not the cause* (split-brain interpreter,
Gazzaniga; "telling more than we can know", Nisbett & Wilson). The subject **avows** a belief
they did not act from → dissociates sincere assertion (M9) from the operative disposition.
Challenges verbal-report evidence for belief.

## L6 · False memory · SUPPORTED
A vividly "remembered", believed proposition with **no veridical source** (Loftus,
misinformation effect). Satisfies belief-criteria fully; is *about the past* and *provenance-
mistaken* → sits on the belief/memory boundary and shows belief can be *manufactured*.

## L7 · Bayesian prior · SUPPORTED (formal cognitive science)
A credence held *before* evidence. Is a prior a belief? It is graded, updatable, action-relevant
(via expected value) → belief-like on the credence model; but it may be a mere *formal starting
point* with no personal endorsement. Liminal between credence, assumption, and belief.

## L8 · Value function / expected-value estimate · LIKELY (as a formal construct)
In formal learning theory, an estimate of expected future reward. It **guides action** and
**updates on evidence** (belief-like) but is *procedural/implicit* and carries no explicit
propositional content → sits on the belief/policy/skill boundary. (Recovered as a
cognitive-modelling construct; no system evaluated.)

## L9 · Predictive-processing posterior · SUPPORTED (framework)
A brain's probabilistic estimate of hidden causes, minimizing prediction error. Called "belief"
in the framework; sub-personal, graded, automatic → collapses belief into expectation (see
NEIGHBOR_CONCEPTS dispute). Liminal between belief and expectation *by construction*.

## L10 · Religious belief / faith · SUPPORTED
Van Leeuwen and others argue "religious credence" behaves **differently** from mundane factual
belief: less evidence-vulnerable, more identity- and practice-laden, sometimes explicitly held
"on faith". Debate: is it *belief*, or *acceptance / a distinct attitude*? Strains
evidence-sensitivity and direction-of-fit.

## L11 · Scientific belief / theory-acceptance · SUPPORTED
Held provisionally, with explicit uncertainty, often as *acceptance of a theory for use* rather
than belief-true (van Fraassen: one may **accept** a theory as empirically adequate without
**believing** it true). Liminal between belief, credence, and acceptance/hypothesis.

## L12 · Animal belief · SUPPORTED (long-standing debate)
Can a languageless creature believe? **Davidson: no** (belief requires the concept of
belief/error, which requires language). Others (Allen, Bermúdez, and the caching/deception
data) grant **proto-** or **non-conceptual** belief. Liminal on the belief/expectation and
belief/behaviour-disposition boundary.

## L13 · Infant cognition · SUPPORTED
Pre-verbal false-belief looking-time results (Onishi & Baillargeon) suggest belief-like
representation before language; the **rich vs lean** interpretation debate contests whether this
is *belief*-representation or a simpler expectation/behaviour-rule. Liminal by the same cut as L12.

## What the catalogue shows (SUPPORTED)
- **Every criterion of belief is failed by *some* accepted-as-belief-adjacent case:** endorsement
  (L1, L9), evidence-sensitivity (L3, L4, L10), action-guidance (L4), veridical provenance (L6),
  propositional/conceptual content (L8, L12), personal-level access (L2, L9).
- **No single case fails *all* criteria** — each sits on a *different* edge. → belief's periphery
  is populated by cases that share *overlapping* subsets of the core, not a common essence. This
  is the empirical basis for the family-resemblance question (FAMILY_RESEMBLANCE) — the liminal
  cases are the data that any essentialist definition must exclude, and every attempted essence
  excludes some case that experts still call belief-adjacent.
