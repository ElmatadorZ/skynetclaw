# Red-Team of Causality — can belief be shown to have no causal power?

> Attack every position that grants belief causal status. Try to prove belief is causally inert.
> Destroy weak arguments (on either side). Then the final question. Tags: SUPPORTED / LIKELY /
> SPECULATIVE / UNKNOWN / FALSIFIED / RETRACTED.

## ATTACK 1 — Kim's exclusion: the physical does all the work · LANDS on non-reductivism, not on belief
- **Attack:** a sufficient physical cause + closure + no overdetermination ⇒ belief is excluded.
- **What dies:** naive **non-reductive** mental causation (belief as an *extra* pusher on top of
  the physical) → **FALSIFIED** as extra-cause.
- **What survives:** (a) **reductive/realization** — belief *is* (realized by) the physical cause,
  so it causes *as* that state (no double-counting); (b) **special-science parity** — the same
  argument would make temperature, genes, and prices epiphenomenal, a *reductio* most reject
  (List & Menzies). So exclusion refutes a *picture* of belief, not belief's difference-making.

## ATTACK 2 — Correlation, not causation: belief just co-varies with behaviour · DOES NOT LAND
- **Attack:** belief-behaviour links are observational correlations.
- **Counter:** the interventional evidence (persuasion, false-feedback, framing) shows
  do(belief) **changes** behaviour with confounds controlled (CS-1), and belief **screens off**
  the stimulus (CS-2). By Woodward's criterion that *is* causation. → correlation-only claim
  **FALSIFIED** for the belief-state.

## ATTACK 3 — Clever confound: the manipulation changes the vehicle, not "the belief" · LANDS as re-description, not denial
- **Attack:** persuasion changes brain states; you intervened on the vehicle, calling it "belief."
- **Counter:** yes — and the belief *is* (realized by) that state; intervening on it *is*
  intervening on the belief (ATTACK 1's reductive escape). The attack **re-describes** the cause,
  it does not remove it. It **does** land on **content-vs-vehicle** (below), not on causal status.

## ATTACK 4 — Content epiphenomenalism: the *content* rides free (Stich) · SURVIVES (unresolved)
- **Attack:** the syntactic vehicle causes; the semantic content is along for the ride.
- **Status:** **not refuted.** No clean intervention varies content while holding vehicle fixed
  (CS "content-causation" = UNKNOWN). Dretske's structuring-cause reply (content explains
  behaviour-*type*) is **LIKELY**, not decisive. → **content-inertness survives as UNKNOWN**;
  **state-efficacy does not (it's SUPPORTED).**

## ATTACK 5 — Eliminativism: there are no beliefs to be causes (Churchland) · LANDS only if the level is empty
- **Attack:** folk psychology is false; belief refers to nothing; a-fortiori causes nothing.
- **Counter:** requires that **no** projectible, multiply-realized belief-level regularity
  survives reduction — but such regularities are exactly what the special sciences trade in
  (Fodor). As long as belief-level generalizations are **robust under intervention and invisible
  to any single lower description**, the level is populated and causal. Eliminativism is a *bet on
  future neuroscience*, currently **UNKNOWN**, not established.

## ATTACK 6 — Interventionism is too permissive: it makes everything a cause · PARTIALLY LANDS
- **Attack:** if any do(X)→ΔY counts, "the position of Jupiter" could be a "cause"; the criterion
  over-generates, so "belief causes" is cheap.
- **Counter:** **proportionality/stability** (CS-3, D3) filters this — a cause must be the
  *difference-maker at the right grain* over a *stable* range; idle variables fail invariance.
  The criterion is disciplined, not anything-goes. Attack **downgrades** interventionism's
  strength from "trivially settles it" to "settles it with a proportionality constraint" (LIKELY).

## Net result
| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Belief (the **state**) is causally inert | **FALSIFIED** (do(belief) changes behaviour) |
| Belief is a **non-reductive extra** pusher | **FALSIFIED** (Kim exclusion) |
| Belief causes **as a realized/difference-making** state | **SUPPORTED** (interventionism + special-science parity) |
| Belief-**content** (qua semantics) is efficacious | **LIKELY / UNKNOWN** (content-vs-vehicle untested) |
| There are **no** beliefs (eliminativism) | **UNKNOWN** (bet on future reduction) |

**Can belief be shown to have no causal power?** For the belief-**state**: **no** — the attempt
fails against interventional evidence. For belief-**content-qua-content**: **not shown either
way** — it is the one live inertness (UNKNOWN). So the strongest defensible skeptical position is
narrow: *"the semantic content may ride on the vehicle"* — everything broader (state inertness,
eliminativism-as-fact) is unproven or falsified.

## FINAL QUESTION — If removing belief changes no prediction and no intervention outcome, does science still require the concept?

**No — and this is exactly the operative test.** A concept earns its place in science by making a
difference to either **prediction** or **intervention** (the two warrants; the prediction/causation
firewall). A concept that changes **neither** is an **idle wheel**: it has no instrumental value
(fails prediction) and no causal value (fails intervention), so **parsimony (Occam / van Fraassen)
licenses its removal** — this is precisely the eliminativist conclusion *in that scenario*.
**SUPPORTED** as a conditional.

**But the antecedent is empirically false for real minds.** Removing/manipulating belief **does**
change intervention outcomes (belief-manipulation studies) **and** belief-talk **does** improve
prediction (the intentional stance beats null models). So the concept is **required** — not by
tradition, but because it currently **pays its way in both currencies.** **SUPPORTED.**

**The surviving principle (SUPPORTED):**
> Science requires the concept of belief **exactly to the extent that belief makes a predictive or
> interventional difference — no more, no less.** Strip both differences and belief becomes an
> eliminable posit (the counterfactual eliminativists are right about). Keep either difference and
> belief is indispensable *for that reason*. Belief's licence is **difference-making, not
> metaphysical honour** — which is why the honest live debate has narrowed to a single unresolved
> point: whether the difference is made by the belief's **content** or only by its **vehicle**
> (UNKNOWN).
