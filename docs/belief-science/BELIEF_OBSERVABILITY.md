# Belief Observability — how belief is detected in practice

> Given the prior result (belief = *stable disposition*, not a stored object), how is that
> disposition *empirically detected*? Recovering the actual detection methods used across
> the sciences of mind. Tags: SUPPORTED (canonical method in its field) / LIKELY / SPECULATIVE
> / UNKNOWN / FALSIFIED. Science first; no software, no Genesis.

## The observability problem, stated
A disposition is a *counterfactual* (would-act-as-if-P). Counterfactuals are not directly
observed; they are inferred from **patterned actual behaviour across manipulated conditions.**
Every method below is a way to make a counterfactual leave an actual trace.

## Detection methods (recovered)

### M1 · Revealed preference / betting elicitation · SUPPORTED (de Finetti, Ramsey; economics)
Offer choices/bets whose payoff depends on P; the accepted odds *reveal* the credence. Belief
is read off decisions, not introspection. Coherence (no Dutch book) is the consistency check.

### M2 · Intentional-stance triangulation (belief–desire–action) · SUPPORTED (Dennett)
Attribute the belief–desire pair that best predicts goal-directed action; belief is inferred
as the premise that, with the imputed goal, rationalizes the behaviour across situations.

### M3 · Violation-of-expectation (VoE) · SUPPORTED (Spelke, Baillargeon; developmental psych)
Show a possible vs an *impossible* (expectation-violating) event; **longer looking** at the
impossible one is taken as evidence the subject *expected/believed* otherwise. The canonical
non-verbal belief/expectation probe (used on pre-verbal infants).

### M4 · False-belief task · SUPPORTED (Wimmer & Perner; Sally–Anne)
The gold standard that isolates *belief* from mere reality-tracking: the subject must predict
an agent who holds a belief **contradicting the current world** (object moved while agent
absent). Success requires representing a *belief as a belief* (theory of mind). Non-verbal
variants: anticipatory looking (Onishi & Baillargeon; Krupenye et al. in great apes).

### M5 · Prediction consistency / proper scoring · SUPPORTED (Bayesian, forecasting)
Score predictions with log/Brier rules across many related items; a belief shows as a
*coherent, calibrated* pattern of predictions, not item-by-item guesses.

### M6 · Error-driven update / learning curves · SUPPORTED (Rescorla–Wagner; predictive processing)
Track how responses change after evidence: belief is inferred from the *direction and
magnitude of updating* (prediction-error-scaled). A state that updates rationally on evidence
behaves like a belief; one that doesn't, like a fixed reflex.

### M7 · Counterfactual & causal probing · SUPPORTED (Pearl's ladder: association→intervention→counterfactual)
Ask/observe "under intervention do(X), and had-X-been counterfactuals": a believer answers the
higher rungs *consistently* with a model; correlation-only systems fail rung 2/3.

### M8 · Planning / means-ends coherence · SUPPORTED (folk-psychology, AI planning)
Novel instrumental action toward a goal, adjusted when the path is blocked, implies a belief
about the world's structure (the plan presupposes believed preconditions/effects).

### M9 · Sincere assertion (linguistic) · SUPPORTED (speech-act theory; Moore's paradox)
For language-users, sincere assertion of P is evidence of belief that P — anchored by Moore's
paradox ("P, but I don't believe P" is pragmatically defective), which ties assertion to
belief. Weak alone (assertion can be insincere), strong in combination.

### M10 · Metacognition / uncertainty monitoring · SUPPORTED (comparative psych; Smith et al.)
Opt-out / confidence responses under uncertainty (studied in primates, rats, birds) index a
*graded* belief-like state (the subject "knows it doesn't know").

### M11 · Physiological / neural correlates · SUPPORTED as method / **NOT black-box**
N400 (semantic expectation), mismatch negativity, dopaminergic reward-prediction-error, fMRI
expectation signals. Detects belief-like states but requires internal access → excluded from a
black-box protocol (flagged for BLACK_BOX_PROTOCOL).

### M12 · Ethological cue-sensitive tests · SUPPORTED (Clayton & Emery scrub-jay caching; tactical deception)
Species-appropriate tasks where behaviour (e.g., re-caching food only when *observed* by a
competitor) implies a belief about another's mental state.

## What every method has in common (SUPPORTED)
All infer belief from **behaviour under manipulated/counterfactual conditions** — never from a
single actual response, and never (except M11) from internals. Detection = *make the
disposition's counterfactual profile show up in actual data.* The strongest methods (M4 VoE/
false-belief, M6 updating, M7 counterfactual) work precisely because they probe **conditions
the system was not simply trained/told to answer** — anticipating the false-positive problem
(FALSE_POSITIVES).

## Scope limit (SUPPORTED)
These methods detect *that a belief-like disposition is present and roughly what its content
is*. They do **not** deliver: truth (external), justification (external), or phenomenal
"what-it's-like" (M11 at best correlates). Observability is of the **disposition**, per the
prior mission's surviving definition.
