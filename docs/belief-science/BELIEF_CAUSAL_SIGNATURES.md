# Belief Causal Signatures — consequences unique to *causal* belief

> What does a *causal* belief leave behind that a merely *predictive/descriptive* belief-summary
> does not? These separate PREDICTION from CAUSATION (OBJECTIVE 3). Grounded in interventionist
> causation (Woodward), mediation (Pearl), and difference-making/proportionality (Yablo; List &
> Menzies). Tags: SUPPORTED / LIKELY / SPECULATIVE / UNKNOWN.

## The core asymmetry (SUPPORTED)
A **correlational/descriptive** belief-summary predicts behaviour *as things actually run* but
**need not survive intervention**. A **causal** belief-behaviour relation is **invariant under
intervention**. Therefore every causal signature below is a form of *"the relation holds even
when you push on it."* Prediction that breaks under intervention is not causation.

## CS-1 · Invariance under intervention · SUPPORTED (the master signature — Woodward)
Manipulate the belief directly (induce/remove it); behaviour changes **in the predicted
direction, across a range of backgrounds.** A mere predictive summary can be *stable in
observation yet collapse under do()*. Only a causal belief yields **stable** do-behaviour
coupling. *Unique to causation.*

## CS-2 · Mediation / screening-off · SUPPORTED (Pearl; Baron–Kenny→modern causal mediation)
In stimulus → **belief** → response, conditioning on the belief **screens off** the stimulus
from the response (renders them conditionally independent), and an intervention on the stimulus
loses its effect when the belief is held fixed. A non-causal summary does not screen off. *This
is the observable of "belief is on the causal path."*

## CS-3 · Proportionality / correct-grain difference-making · SUPPORTED (Yablo; List & Menzies)
The **belief-level** variable is the difference-maker: changing the belief changes behaviour,
while changing **belief-irrelevant physical details** (different realizer, same belief) does
**not**. The cause sits at the grain that *makes the difference* — often the belief, not the
micro-physical detail (a higher-level cause can be *more* proportional). A descriptive summary
carries no commitment about grain.

## CS-4 · Counterfactual dependence at the content's grain · SUPPORTED (Lewis-style, made interventional)
Behaviour counterfactually depends on the **specific content**: had the belief been about ¬P,
the (content-appropriate) behaviour would differ. Distinguishes a *content-efficacious* belief
from one whose behaviour rides on a confound.

## CS-5 · Targeted-disruption dissociation · SUPPORTED (lesion/ablation; double dissociation)
Disrupting the substrate that realizes the belief produces a **specific** behavioural deficit,
while leaving belief-irrelevant behaviour intact (and the converse for another substrate). Double
dissociation is the neuroscience signature that a state is *doing work*, not riding along.

## CS-6 · Stability across contexts (autonomy of the higher-level law) · LIKELY (Fodor special sciences)
The belief→behaviour regularity is **projectible and multiply realizable** — it holds across
physically diverse instances. A regularity visible *only* at the belief grain (invisible to any
single lower-level description) is evidence the belief-level does **autonomous** causal work.

## What these exclude (the prediction/causation firewall) · SUPPORTED
| Signature present? | Then the belief is at least… | Rules out |
|---|---|---|
| CS-1 invariance-under-intervention | a **cause** (Woodward) | mere correlation (P5 pure instrumentalism, P9 epiphenomenalism-of-state) |
| CS-2 mediation/screening | **on the causal path** | a downstream by-product of a common cause |
| CS-3 proportionality | a **correct-grain** cause | the claim that only micro-physics causes |
| CS-5 dissociation | a **realized, working** state | a purely fictional posit |

## The one signature that stays out of reach · UNKNOWN
**Content-vs-vehicle efficacy.** Every signature above can be satisfied by the belief *state*
without settling whether its **content** (semantics) or its **vehicle** (syntax/physics) is the
efficacious property, because interventions that change content typically change the vehicle
too. Isolating content-causation would require an intervention that **varies content while
holding the vehicle fixed** — **not known to be realizable.** So *state-causation* has SUPPORTED
signatures; *content-causation* has only LIKELY ones (Dretske's structuring-cause argument), and
its clean experimental signature is **UNKNOWN**.

## Summary (SUPPORTED)
> Causal belief ≠ predictive belief precisely in **CS-1 (invariance under intervention)** and
> **CS-2 (mediation)**. A belief that predicts behaviour but whose relation to behaviour
> **breaks when you intervene** is descriptive, not causal. A belief whose relation **survives
> intervention and mediates the stimulus** is causal — regardless of whether one can further
> isolate content from vehicle.
