# 01 — Axioms · Inference Rules · Conditions

> Pure epistemology. Deliverables 2 (Axioms) & 3 (Inference Rules) + Questions 2
> (necessary conditions) & 3 (sufficient conditions). Recovered, falsifiable, tagged.

---

## Deliverable 2 · Axioms of warrant

The minimal principles most theories share (across internalist/externalist lines),
stated so each is refutable by a case.

- **A1 · Ground axiom.** Warrant requires a ground: `W(S,b,g,π) → g ≠ ∅`. A belief
  warranted by *nothing* is a contradiction in terms. (Refuted by: a warranted
  groundless belief. Candidate counterexample — self-evident axioms; reply: their
  ground is their self-evidence, not nothing.) SUPPORTED.
- **A2 · Truth-aim axiom.** The purpose `π` is truth (accuracy), not comfort,
  utility, or coherence-for-its-own-sake. A process aimed at anything else yields
  something other than warrant. (Refuted by: warrant that survives being aimed away
  from truth.) SUPPORTED, with the pragmatist dissent noted (James — file 04).
- **A3 · Degree axiom.** Warrant is monotone in ground-strength, *ceteris paribus*:
  stronger ground ⇒ at least as much warrant — **but only holding defeaters fixed**
  (the *ceteris paribus* is doing heavy work; A3 is NOT monotone in raw evidence
  quantity — see file 04, the non-monotonicity theorem). LIKELY.
- **A4 · Defeasibility axiom.** Any a-posteriori warrant can be defeated by further
  information; no finite empirical ground is indefeasible. (Refuted by: an empirical
  belief no evidence could touch.) SUPPORTED (Pollock; fallibilism).
- **A5 · Non-transitivity-of-source axiom.** Warrant does not pass through a chain
  undiminished (file 03). SUPPORTED.
- **A6 · Total-evidence axiom.** Warrant is assessed on *all* the subject's relevant
  evidence, not a chosen subset (Carnap's requirement of total evidence). Cherry-
  picked support is not warrant. SUPPORTED.

## Deliverable 3 · Inference rules (how warrant moves through reasoning)

Warrant is not classical entailment — it is **defeasible**. The rules:

- **R1 · Deductive transmission (weakest-link).** If `g` warrants premises and the
  inference is valid, warrant transmits to the conclusion — but bounded by the
  *least*-warranted premise (min, not product; file 02). Caveat: transmission can
  *fail* even for valid arguments (transmission-failure, Wright/Davies — e.g.
  question-begging inferences where the conclusion is presupposed by warrant for a
  premise). LIKELY.
- **R2 · Ampliative (non-monotonic) support.** Inductive/abductive inference confers
  *defeasible* warrant: adding premises can *withdraw* a previously warranted
  conclusion (non-monotonicity — the formal signature of defeasible reasoning).
  SUPPORTED (default logics; Pollock; Reiter).
- **R3 · Defeat.** A defeater removes warrant (file 05). Two kinds (Pollock):
  *rebutting* (evidence for ¬p) and *undercutting* (attacks the link g→b without
  bearing on p). SUPPORTED.
- **R4 · Closure (restricted).** Warrant is *not* freely closed under known
  entailment — the sceptical closure debates (Dretske/Nozick deny; Williamson
  restricts). Believing p warrantedly and knowing p⊢q does not automatically warrant
  q (esp. across sensitivity/safety). CONTESTED — one of the field's live faults.

## Q2 · Necessary conditions — which candidates survive

Tested against the standard cases (Gettier, demon, clairvoyant, barn-façade):

| Candidate | Necessary? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Truth-connection** (reliability / sensitivity / safety) | **LIKELY necessary** — some link to truth (a reliable process, or "couldn't easily be wrong") survives most cases; its *form* is disputed (safety vs sensitivity vs reliabilism) | keep, form open |
| **Evidence / accessible grounds** | **NOT uncontroversially necessary** — externalism (reliabilism) grants warrant to a reliable process with no accessible evidence (the reluctant clairvoyant tests this both ways) | CONTESTED |
| **No undefeated defeater** | **LIKELY necessary** — nearly all theories require the absence of undefeated defeaters | keep |
| **Counterfactual sensitivity / safety** | **candidate** — "if p were false, S would not believe p" (Nozick) or "S could not easily believe falsely" (Sosa/Williamson); safety currently favored | keep (safety) |
| **Calibration** | necessary for *graded* warrant (credences), not for flat belief | scope-limited |
| **Coherence** | necessary per coherentism, denied by foundationalism | CONTESTED |
| **Reality (a true world-connection)** | folds into truth-connection; not an extra condition | subsumed |

**Recovered synthesis (LIKELY):** the *stable core* of necessary conditions is
**{ truth-connection, defeat-freedom }**; everything else (evidence-accessibility,
coherence, specific counterfactual form) is theory-relative. No single condition in
the table is uncontested except, arguably, defeat-freedom.

## Q3 · Sufficient conditions — do any exist?

**Recovered answer: no known finite set of conditions is jointly sufficient for
warrant (⇒ knowledge).** This is not ignorance of the answer; it is a *result* — the
sixty-year record since Gettier (1963): every proposed analysis
(JTB, causal, defeasibility, sensitivity, safety, reliabilist, virtue) has met a
counterexample. Two live responses:

- **Knowledge-first (Williamson):** knowledge/warrant is *unanalysable* — a
  primitive, not a conjunction of simpler conditions. Then "sufficient conditions"
  is the wrong request. LIKELY.
- **Cluster/no-essence:** warrant is a homeostatic cluster (as belief was, in the
  belief-science recovery) — sufficiency holds only *for the core*, degrades at the
  periphery, no sharp sufficient set. LIKELY.

Either way the recovered claim is the same and is **falsifiable**: *produce a finite
set of individually-necessary, jointly-sufficient, counterexample-proof conditions
for warrant.* Six decades of failure make this SUPPORTED-as-the-field's-state, but a
single successful analysis would refute it — which is exactly what keeps it
scientific rather than dogmatic.

> The parallel to the belief-science result is exact and not accidental: belief had
> "no classical essence"; warrant has "no sufficient analysis." Both are the same
> fact — the epistemic kinds are cluster kinds, individuated by a family of
> conditions no one of which is the essence.
