The mission's question — *what gives a system the licence to believe something* —
is, in the field, the question of **warrant**: the term of art (Plantinga) for
"that which, in sufficient quantity, transforms true belief into knowledge." It is
the missing third thing after truth and belief. This file recovers what kind of
thing it is.

---

## Q1 · What is a warrant (minimal ontology)

Not a definition (Q3 shows a clean definition is probably unavailable) but the
smallest ontology the concept requires.

**W1 — Warrant is a normative property, not a causal or psychological one.** It is
a *licence* / *permission* / *entitlement* to hold a doxastic attitude — an
"ought/may", not an "is". A belief can be *caused* by a process yet be *unwarranted*
(wishful thinking causes belief without licensing it). SUPPORTED (the is/ought gap;
the deontological strand from Locke→Chisholm→Plantinga).

**W2 — Warrant is a relation, not a monadic property.** Minimal form:
```
W( S, b, g, π )   —  subject S is warranted in doxastic attitude b, in virtue of
                     ground-structure g, relative to epistemic purpose π (truth).
```
Drop any argument and the concept degenerates: a belief is not warranted *simpliciter*
but warranted *by* something *for* an aim. SUPPORTED (relational; the "in virtue of"
is common to evidentialism and reliabilism alike — they dispute what `g` is, not
that there is a `g`).

**W3 — Warrant is fallible (truth-independent in the token, truth-aimed in the
type).** A warranted belief can be false; a lucky true belief can be unwarranted
(Gettier). So warrant ≠ truth. Yet warrant is *aimed* at truth — its point (`π`) is
getting things right. This severs warrant from truth while keeping them oriented.
SUPPORTED (fallibilism; Gettier).

**W4 — Warrant is degreed and defeasible.** It comes in amounts (a hunch < strong
evidence) and can be *gained and lost* over time without the belief's content
changing (defeaters — file 03). SUPPORTED.

**W5 — Warrant is distinct from its indicators.** *Having* warrant ≠ *believing one
has* warrant. Externalism (reliabilism) allows warrant without access to it; internalism
demands the ground be cognitively accessible. Whether `g` must be accessible to `S`
is the internalism/externalism axis — CONTESTED, and the deepest unresolved fault
line under the whole concept.

### Warrant vs its four neighbors (the boundary — recovered)
| Concept | Relation to warrant |
|---|---|
| **Truth** | orthogonal in the token (W3); warrant's *aim*, not its *content* |
| **Confidence / credence** | a *psychological* degree; warrant is its *normative* counterpart — how confident one is *permitted* to be. Miscalibration = confidence ≠ warrant |
| **Justification** | near-synonym in much usage; Plantinga separates them — *justification* = deontological blamelessness (did your duty), *warrant* = the truth-connecting property that yields knowledge. One can be justified yet unwarranted (a blameless victim of an evil demon) |
| **Knowledge** | warrant is a *component*: knowledge ≈ warranted true belief (+ anti-Gettier). Warrant is necessary for knowledge by construction (W-necessity, file 04) |

## Q4 · Taxonomy of warrant (by ground-structure `g`)

Warrant is not one kind; kinds differ by the *ground* that supplies them, and each
kind has a different default profile for decay (file 03), transfer (03), and
composition (02). The primary cut is **a priori vs a posteriori**.

| Kind | Ground `g` | A priori? | Default decay | Default transfer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Logical** | validity / form | yes | none (barring found error) | by proof |
| **Mathematical** | proof from axioms | yes | none (barring found error) | by proof / re-derivation |
| **Empirical** | direct observation | no | with new observation | by re-observation |
| **Experimental** | controlled intervention | no | with failed replication | by **reproduction** |
| **Statistical** | frequencies / samples | no | with sampling/model shift | by data + method |
| **Predictive** | a model's forecast track record | no | with each miss (calibration) | by the model's record |
| **Historical** | traces / records of the past | no | with new record / reinterpretation | by archive access |
| **Testimonial** | another's assertion | inherited | with source-reliability change | by the transmission chain (03) |

Two structural facts about the taxonomy (recovered):
- **A priori warrant is transfer-by-proof and effectively decay-free** — its ground
  does not go stale (2+2=4 is not weakened by tomorrow). A posteriori warrant is
  time-indexed and defeasible. This asymmetry (SUPPORTED) drives files 03/05.
- **Testimonial warrant is not a source but a *transfer* — it has no ground of its
  own**; it borrows another's warrant through a channel, and is therefore the
  lossiest kind (file 03, Q6). SUPPORTED (Coady; the testimony literature).

## What has warrant (the bearer)
The primary bearer is a **doxastic attitude of a subject toward a proposition, via
a ground** — belief or graded credence, not the proposition alone (a proposition is
true or false, not warranted). Derivatively: methods, sources, and *systems* can be
called warranted as shorthand for "reliably producing warranted attitudes."
SUPPORTED. Whether degreed credences or flat beliefs are the fundamental bearer is
itself contested (traditional epistemology: belief; Bayesian: credence) — CONTESTED,
inherited from the belief-vehicle debate.

## Falsifiers for this ontology
- W1 is refuted if a purely causal account with no normative remainder fully
  captures ordinary warrant-ascriptions (strong naturalism's wager) — not yet
  achieved. W2 is refuted by a coherent monadic warrant with no "in virtue of".
  W3 is refuted if all warranted beliefs turn out true (they do not — fallibilism).
  Each claim above names a case that would overturn it; a claim without one has been
  demoted to CONTESTED.
