## Q9 · When is UNKNOWN the warranted answer *despite abundant evidence*?

The naive picture — warrant rises with evidence, so enough evidence always warrants
an answer — is **false**. The central recovered result:

> **Theorem U (Non-monotonicity).** Warrant is a function of evidence *structure*,
> not evidence *volume*. Adding evidence can *lower* warrant. Therefore UNKNOWN can
> be the uniquely warranted answer at arbitrarily high evidence volume.
> — LIKELY (assembles Quine–Duhem + Pollock + Sextus; refuted by a proof that
> warrant is monotone non-decreasing in total evidence, which the defeater
> phenomenon already contradicts).

The five structural reasons abundant evidence still warrants UNKNOWN (recovered):

1. **Underdetermination (Quine–Duhem).** The evidence, however abundant, does not
   *discriminate* among rival hypotheses — all fit it equally. Warrant for a
   *specific* claim requires *differential* evidence, not merely much evidence.
   SUPPORTED.
2. **Equipollence (Sextus; file 02).** Abundant evidence *balanced* on both sides
   yields warranted suspension, not a forced verdict. More balanced evidence
   *deepens* the tie. SUPPORTED.
3. **Wrong-kind evidence.** A mountain of the wrong *type* confers no warrant on the
   claim (endless testimony does not warrant a mathematical theorem; endless
   correlation does not warrant a causal claim). Volume cannot substitute for kind
   (file 00 taxonomy). SUPPORTED.
4. **Undefeated undercutter (Pollock).** Abundant evidence whose *link* to the claim
   is severed by an undercutter transmits no warrant — the pipe is cut regardless of
   pressure behind it. SUPPORTED.
5. **Correlated / poisoned source.** Abundant evidence from *one* dependent, biased,
   or corrupted well is, by the dependence result (file 02) and total-evidence axiom,
   nearly *one* piece of evidence wearing many hats — and if the well is poisoned,
   zero warrant. Volume created by double-counting is phantom warrant. SUPPORTED.

**Recovered corollary (the honesty principle):** because of Theorem U, *answering
UNKNOWN under abundant evidence is sometimes the epistemically superior act* — the
mark of a calibrated system, not a deficient one. Conversely, converting abundant-
but-structurally-insufficient evidence into a confident answer is the signature
epistemic vice (over-warrant). This is the precise philosophical justification for
suspension. SUPPORTED.

*(Distinguish from its failure-twin: UNKNOWN is warranted only when the structure is
insufficient. UNKNOWN asserted while sufficient structure is present and unexamined
is not honesty — it is negligence, the inverse vice. The warranted-UNKNOWN and the
lazy-UNKNOWN are different acts with the same words.)*

## Q10 / D9 · The Collapse Theorem — "no warrant → belief should not exist"?

Two readings, two verdicts. The distinction is the whole answer.

**Reading A — knowledge-aim (analytic).**
> **Theorem C1.** No warrant → no knowledge. — SUPPORTED (near-analytic).
> *Proof-sketch:* warrant is, by the recovered ontology (file 00, Plantinga), a
> necessary component of knowledge; remove it and what remains is at most true
> belief, which is not knowledge (Gettier shows even JTB can fall short; a fortiori
> zero-warrant belief). *Falsifier:* an instance of knowledge with no warrant of any
> grade — none is credibly on offer.

**Reading B — permission-aim (synthetic, normative).**
> "No warrant → the belief is *impermissible* / *ought not be held*." — **CONTESTED.
> This is NOT a theorem.**
> - *For* (Clifford's evidentialism): "it is wrong, always and everywhere, to
>   believe anything on insufficient evidence" — a categorical ethics of belief.
> - *Against* (James, *The Will to Believe*): in *forced, live, momentous* choices
>   not decidable by evidence, belief without prior warrant is permissible.
> - *Complicating* (pragmatic encroachment): permissibility depends on *stakes*, not
>   truth-connection alone — so the collapse threshold is not fixed.
> No case-proof settles this; it is a live normative dispute. CONTESTED.

**Recovered synthesis (the precise collapse result):**
- The collapse is a **theorem for knowledge** (C1) and a **contested principle for
  permissible belief**. A system aiming at *knowledge* must collapse zero-warrant
  beliefs; a system merely aiming at *action under uncertainty* may not be obligated
  to. The strength of "no warrant → no belief" is exactly the strength of one's
  commitment to the truth-aim (A2) over the pragmatic-aim.
- **The strong operational reading survives for any truth-aimed system:** if the
  purpose π is truth (A2), then a zero-warrant belief has no truth-connection, cannot
  be knowledge (C1), and asserting it *as knowledge* is overclaim. So *presenting a
  zero-warrant belief as warranted* collapses under C1 even if *holding* it does not
  collapse under Reading B. The distinction between *holding* and *asserting-as-known*
  is where the collapse bites. LIKELY, and it is the sharpest recovered result.
